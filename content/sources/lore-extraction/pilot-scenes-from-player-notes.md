# Pilot Scenes from Brigit's Player Notes

**Source**: Brigit's chapter notes (Chapters 5-16) and session summaries (0-15)
**Purpose**: Scene-by-scene guide for panel art direction and dialogue overlays for Pilot A (Panels 1-80)
**Cross-referenced with**: `pilot-story-bible.md` panel breakdown

---

## How to Use This Document

The pilot covers the party's FIRST session: signing up for the Scarlet Tunic Brigade without remembering why, being led into the sewers by Jorgen, and discovering the ritual chamber with the Raven Queen's phylactery. Brigit's notes and the summaries provide the raw player experience -- the surprise, humor, fear, and chaos that should inform how each panel feels.

Each section below maps to a pilot Act and provides:
- Vivid details from the actual game sessions
- Exact dialogue and player reactions
- Emotional texture that should inform art direction
- Specific visual details Brigit noticed

## ACT 1: The Summons (Panels 1-7)

**Story Bible**: Conquis Abbey receives military summons. Riders prepare to depart.

### Player Notes Context

The abbey and summons are pre-campaign framing -- Brigit's notes begin at the recruitment yard in Argos. However, the **Summary 0 and 1** establishes critical emotional context:

**Scene: The Mystery of the Contract**
> "Our adventurers found themselves having signed up for a contract as new recruits of the Scarlet Tunic Brigade, despite not remembering when or why."

- **Art Direction**: This sense of lost agency should haunt Panel 7 (the solitary figure in the abbey window). The character staring out doesn't fully understand why they feel compelled to go. Something is already wrong.
- **Emotional Beat**: Not heroic resolve -- confused obligation. The body is moving before the mind catches up.

---

## ACT 2: The Journey (Panels 8-14)

**Story Bible**: Riders travel from abbey to port town. Environmental storytelling, escalating unease.

### Player Notes Context

No direct journey notes (the campaign started in medias res in Argos), but the summaries describe what the landscape AROUND Argos looks like:

**Scene: The Blighted Land**
- From Summary 7: The Old Forest is dangerous enough that Finkle needed a full ship (the Thornberry Shade) to bypass it
- Undead attack Argos every 4 hours, alternating North and East gates
- From Summary 9: Gorgon markings outside caves near the town -- tusks covered in thin chalky substance, territorial scrapes on cliff faces
- From Chapter 9: A divination prophecy hints at what's coming:
  > "The anchors fall, the realms asunder / Hearts of stone gathered from plunder / Death incarnate knocks at the door / Below the blood star begins the final war"

- **Art Direction for Panels 10-11**: The abandoned merchant cart with claw marks should evoke the Gorgon territory markings -- parallel scratches, chalky residue. The town they're approaching is under siege from something it can barely fight.

## ACT 3: The Forge (Panels 15-20)

**Story Bible**: Secret forge produces a god-killer blade with supernatural light.

### Player Notes Context

No direct forge scene in early notes, but the aesthetic of Argos's craftsmanship is established:

**Scene: Dolf the Blacksmith**
- From Chapter 11: Dolf is both a weaponsmith and a political figure -- runs "The Iron's Wine" shop
- From Chapter 13: Dolf appears "with his hammer, guards and in his nightie" when called at night
- The craftspeople of Argos are described as wearing "protective gear and their weapons (knitting needles, hammers, forging tools)" -- they are workers who fight

- **Art Direction**: The smith in the forge sequence should have this dual nature -- a craftsperson whose tools serve both creation and war. Dolf's personality (gruff, principled, shows up in his nightgown when needed) can inform the forge smith's posture.

> "Brdige (sp?) transformed into a wolf and sprang between the drow and the door"
> "Bridgite (sp?)... Bgdrite (sp?)... Bdfgdaeaghdt (sp?)"

**From Summary 3-4**:
> "Brigit taking on the form of a giant wolf managed to take a bite out of the undead"

**Character Voice**: Quiet and watchful in human form, but absolutely savage as a wolf. The shift between "hands in her pockets" Brigit and "rips the skin from his arm" wolf-Brigit is the character's core tension. She's more comfortable as an animal. Also: she takes notes on everything (she IS the notetaker).

**Key Visual Detail**: Her wolf form is described as "dire wolf" -- this is not a cute pet wolf, this is a predator.

**Art Direction for Panels 23-26**:
- Panel 23 (forest edge): The wolf should be LEAN and ALERT -- a predator reading the ecosystem, not a dog. The scarred forest should feel like something she's been tracking.
- Panel 24 (the shift): The transformation from wolf to woman should be "liquid and organic" per the story bible. Brigit's human form retains the alert, scanning quality.
- Panel 25 (barefoot walk): Brigit walks through town barefoot, hands at her sides or in pockets. She observes. She does not perform.
- Panel 26 (notice board): She reads the recruitment poster the way a wolf reads a scent trail -- assessing threat, not opportunity.

**Memorable Brigit Moments for Future Reference**:
- "I can't be disarmed." (Chapter 13) -- because her weapon IS her body
- Casting thunderwave on HERSELF to push wights back into her own spike growth (Chapter 11)

## ACT 5: The Descent (Panels 45-61)

### THE SEWER ENTRANCE

**From Summary 0-1**:
> "The new recruits found themselves swept along into the sewers of the Middle Ward."
> "Jorgen pulled a lever on the other end of the hallway and entered the room. Immediately upon entry the door slammed shut and the sewer entrance was sealed."

**Art Direction for Panels 48-49**:
- Jorgen's briefing should have the confidence of a man who has done this before. He HAS done this before -- with his last set of recruits who are now dead.
- The sewer entrance: iron grate, stairs into darkness. The lever Jorgen pulls is on the OTHER side -- he separates himself from the party immediately.

### THE KOBOLD ENCOUNTER (Panels 51-54)

**From Summary 0-1** (the defining scene):
> "Alone in the dark the party light up some torches and revealed a horde of Kobolds."
> "The Kobolds were originally just staring at the party until Daina (not Sprocket), splashed sewer sludge on one of them."
> "Stalwart Little stepped in to intimidate them in Draconic. Something about the Dragonborn was able to keep the Kobolds at bay despite their eerie chanting of 'SCARLET, SCARLET, SCARLET' and following the party down the hall."

**The Chanting**: The kobolds chant "SCARLET, SCARLET, SCARLET" -- they recognize the Scarlet Tunic Brigade uniforms. This is deeply unsettling because it means they've encountered (and killed) brigade members before. Jorgen trained them.

**Art Direction for Panels 51-54**:
- Panel 51: Hundreds of small eyes reflecting torchlight. The kobolds are NOT attacking yet. They're watching.
- Panel 52: Frozen standoff. The chanting "SCARLET" should be visible as text in the panel -- small, repeated, growing louder.
- Panel 53: LOW ANGLE on Stalwart. His Draconic speech is heritage asserting itself. The kobolds respond to the LANGUAGE, not the threat. They recognize a dragon's descendant.
- Panel 54: The kobolds retreat but they retreat RELUCTANTLY. They are not fleeing -- they are yielding to authority they recognize.

### THE TRAP CORRIDOR / CEILING TRAP

**From Summary 0-1**:
> "A large depression in the floor and a blood rune on a button, when activated the blood rune starts a counter of 180s which is counting down until when the ceiling will fall."
> "The party fought the kobolds and discovered another clue also scratched onto a surface, this surface being the rapidly approaching ceiling: 'RUN LEFT'"
> "Stalwart, Nyx, Daina and Brigit ran into the left wall and fade through just in time"

**From Chapter 5**:
> "DM is totally going for a Scooby Doo theme. Motherfuck."
> "Aka we're going to have to split up for clues."
> "DM is totally going to kill us at this point"

**Art Direction for Panels 55-59**:
- The ceiling descending should feel PHYSICALLY OPPRESSIVE. Per story bible: "the KNEEL panel should feel physically oppressive."
- Panel 59 (KNEEL): The word scratched in blood on the ceiling. This is Mordred's humor -- he built this trap as a test, not a defense. He WANTS to see who kneels and who runs.
- The party has to run into a WALL that turns out to be an illusion. Trust nothing in this dungeon.

**Art Direction for Panel 61** (blood light):
- The red light hits them first. This is not torch, not fire -- supernatural. Per the notes: "four endless waterfalls" that "never overflowed despite the endless waterfall."

## CHARACTER DYNAMICS FROM THE NOTES

These relationships should inform panel compositions and body language throughout:

### Stalwart vs. Dallor
Constant physical tension. Stalwart slams Dallor against walls. Dallor talks his way out. From Summary 6: "Stalwart killed two birds with one punch to the face" during a challenge against Dallor. Despite this, Dallor keeps coming back.

In human form: quiet, watchful, "hands in her pockets." As wolf: "bork bork," "bites him in the neck," "rips the skin from his arm." The shift is instantaneous and total. She refers to herself in third person wolf ("the pokemon was confused").

It's practically a character. He uses it for EVERYTHING: pulling levers, holding phylacteries to the ceiling, retrieving torches from under burning zombies. In panels where Sprocket acts, the mage hand should be visible as a faintly glowing ghost hand.

Hedwig the owl is Nyx's primary way of engaging with the world. Sent down chimneys, through doors, into dark hallways. When Hedwig dies (multiple times), Nyx is described as genuinely affected: "Nyx's beloved owl then died a quick and painless death, shrouded in glory and triumph. It died knowing it had done its duty."

## EMOTIONAL TEXTURE: WHAT THE PILOT SHOULD FEEL LIKE

From Brigit's notes, the dominant emotional register of these early sessions is:

1. **Chaos managed by humor**: "DM is totally going to kill us at this point" / "Motherfuck" / "we're going to have to split up for clues"
2. **Plans that barely work**: "SOMEHOW IT WORKED. I don't even know how that worked. SOMETHING WORKED FOR ONCE"
3. **Genuine fear underneath**: "We are totally going to die" / "yeah, we're totally going to die"
4. **Player exhaustion as atmosphere**: "yep I'm done. going to bed" / "I wasn't paying attention" / "I'm sleepy"
5. **Surprise at their own competence**: "first time in however long, we finally feel 100% alive"

The pilot should capture this register: people who are in over their heads, making it work through chaos and stubbornness, and discovering they might actually be heroes -- not because they chose to be, but because they didn't walk away when they could have.
