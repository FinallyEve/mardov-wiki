## Part 1: Contradictions Between DM and Player Sources

### 1.1 The Kobold Provocation -- Who Started It?

**DM Version (Chapter 0 summary):**
> The Kobolds are chanting "SCARLET, SCARLET, SCARLET" but are initially just staring. They are "provoked" but no specific instigator is named.

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> "Daina (not Sprocket), splashed sewer sludge on one of them. Provoked and pissed the Kobolds readied for attack."

The parenthetical "(not Sprocket)" suggests the table argued about who did it. The player summary names Daina definitively.

**Pilot Version:** The pilot omits the provocation entirely. Stalwart's Draconic speech is the first interaction (Panels 51-54).

**CANON FOR PILOT**: The pilot version stands. The kobolds are a threat to be defused, not a comedy beat about sludge-splashing. However, the detail that the kobolds were *passive until provoked* is useful atmosphere -- they were watching, chanting, following. Use the eerie chanting of "SCARLET, SCARLET, SCARLET" in the audio design for Panels 51-52. It is too good to waste.

### 1.2 Jorgen's Role -- Rescuer or Trap-Layer?

**DM Version (Chapter 0):**
> Jorgen is "a human ranger in a gold tabard" who "comes sprinting in pursued by flying kobolds." He is an established Brigade member (gold tunic = veteran). After the kobold fight, Finkle assigns the sewer mission.

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> Jorgen "claimed that the Kobolds had followed him across town from the sewers and had killed his previous set of recruits." Additionally, "Dallor Dallorbill was able to swipe a note written to Jorgen from Finkle" listing the recruits' names, classes, and arrival time -- implying Finkle and Jorgen are coordinating.

The player version adds the stolen note detail, which is critical: Finkle knew exactly who was coming and when. The note makes Jorgen complicit from the beginning -- he is not a random ranger, he is bait.

**Pilot Version:** Jorgen arrives at the tavern (Panel 43) bleeding, with shapes behind him. He leads the party into the sewers and betrays them in the ritual chamber (Panels 67-68).

**CANON FOR PILOT**: Merge. Jorgen's tavern arrival should include the stolen note as a subtle plant. Possible implementation: after the tavern fight (Panel 45 aftermath), Dallor (established as a pickpocket in Panel 31c) produces a note he lifted from the ranger. The note lists their names. Nobody on screen knows what this means yet. The audience will understand on rewatch after the betrayal.

### 1.3 Finkle's Introduction vs. the Pilot's Recruitment

**DM Version (Chapter 0):**
> Finkle is the Brigade's COO. He asks "mysterious questions about ancient cults in the area, particularly the Raven Queen cult." He reveals that cult members "use necromancy and animancy, view death as a steroid, and mark themselves with symbols under their eyelids."

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> "Broden Rootbeard received an inspiration event point and the following information about the Raven Queen Cult" -- confirming one player was particularly engaged with this lore drop.

**Brigit's Chapter 5 Notes:**
> "He's why we don't remember signing up for the brigade" -- Brigit's notes reveal that Mordred manipulated the party into joining. They did not volunteer.

**Pilot Version:** Finkle is a "gnome bureaucrat/drill sergeant" (Panels 35-38) running a recruitment yard. The Raven Queen cult questions are not present. The pilot treats recruitment as straightforward military onboarding.

**CANON FOR PILOT**: The pilot's streamlined Finkle is correct for pacing. However, the "not remembering signing up" detail from Brigit is enormously important for later episodes. Plant it subtly: in Panel 34 (recruits waiting), one character could have a confused expression or whisper "Did you sign up for this?" to another. This becomes a thread for Episode 2.

### 1.4 The Sewer Entrance -- Sealed In or Led Down?

**DM Version (Chapter 0):**
> The party goes to the sewers to collect four sources of ancient magic. The mission is framed as legitimate Brigade work.

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> "Jorgen pulled a lever on the other end of the hallway and entered the room. Immediately upon entry the door slammed shut and the sewer entrance was sealed. The lever when pulled later lead to nothing, as if it had already served its purpose." Jorgen also separated from the party, "slipping his way out of the sight of the Kobolds."

This detail is damning: Jorgen seals them in and slips away. The lever is a one-way mechanism. The party is being herded.

**Pilot Version:** The descent (Panels 48-61) shows Jorgen leading the party down. No explicit sealing mechanism is shown.

**CANON FOR PILOT**: Include the sealed-in moment. After Panel 49 (sewer entrance), add a sound cue: a stone mechanism grinding shut behind them. Jorgen does not react. Nobody notices. The audience does.

### 1.5 The Ritual Chamber -- What Was Actually in It?

**DM Version (Chapter 0):**
> "An ancient ritual site." The party needs to "collect four sources of ancient magic to disperse the kobold infestation." The DM frames the mission as pest control.

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> The ritual chamber has "a massive open chamber with a steeped roof and four endless waterfalls of orange, red, blue and green liquid/pseudo-liquid into a grate below. In the center of the room on top of a massive gear-like pedestal sits a Phylactery of the Raven Queen. Under the pedestal is grooved with tracks from the center to each of the 4 streams. Additionally, there are mysterious markings on the tracks. Along the walls to the room are a total of 12 tunnels to Kobold nests."

**Player Version (Summary 2):**
> Finkle is revealed as "actually a Lich named Mordred." The phylactery "contains a Demi-Lich of current unknown origin" (later revealed as Sabine). The four liquids are more precisely described: "RED blood, hot to the touch; ORANGE mercury mixed with molten iron; BLUE liquid souls; GREEN poison, corrosive like acid."

**Pilot Version:** The ritual chamber (Panel 64) has four waterfalls but recasts them as: blood-red (sacrifice), acid-green (pestilence), mercury-orange (war), soul-blue (death). No phylactery is visible in the pilot. The altar bears the Raven Queen's crest. The sealed steel door with concentric rings is the focus (Panels 71-74).

**CANON FOR PILOT**: The pilot's four-waterfall color system is the adaptation layer. The actual game had the waterfalls as *ingredients* for a necromantic ritual. The pilot reframes them as *symbolic* of the four horsemen/anchors. This is a deliberate creative choice that maps to the endgame Zenith system. Keep the pilot version. The phylactery and Mordred reveal are Episode 2 material.

**DM Version (Chapter 2):**

**CANON FOR PILOT**: The pilot is correct. The Mordred/Finkle reveal is the Episode 2 cliffhanger. The pilot's Jorgen betrayal is a *different* kind of twist -- not "the friendly gnome is actually the big bad" but "the competent rescuer led you here on purpose." This works better for a first episode because it teaches the audience rule #1 of Mardov: trust is earned, not granted by role.

## Part 2: Player Details the DM Missed

### 2.1 Brigit's Internal Voice

Brigit's notes are written in real-time during play, which gives them an immediacy the DM summaries lack.

**Moments of Brigit thinking out loud:**
- "DM is totally going for a Scooby Doo theme. Motherfuck." (Ch 5) -- This shows a player who sees the DM's patterns. For the pilot, this translates to Brigit being the character who reads situations by instinct, the one who knows something is wrong before she can articulate why.
- "Etc Etc, I can't keep track of this." (Ch 5, on the raven room puzzle) -- Brigit's player is overwhelmed by the puzzle complexity. This is useful: in the adaptation, Brigit should NOT be the puzzle-solver. She is the instinct character. She watches, she waits, she acts when the moment comes.
- "DM is totally going to kill us at this point" (Ch 5) -- Genuine player fear. This emotional reality should inform the pilot's descent sequence (Acts 5-6).

### 2.2 The Spelling Running Gag

The player writing Summary 8 (likely Nyx's player) deliberately misspells Brigit's name differently every time: "Bridgite (sp?)", "Brdige (sp?)", "Bgdrite (sp?)", "Bdfgdaeaghdt (sp?)". This is a table joke -- nobody can spell Brigit's name.

**For the pilot**: This is pure gold for character voice. In a future episode where the party is doing paperwork or registration, every form could have her name spelled differently. For the pilot itself, it establishes that Brigit is the character nobody quite knows how to categorize -- even her name resists classification.

### 2.3 Dallor's Relentless Looting

**Player Version (Summary 3-4):**
> "Dallor Dallorbill being the upstanding team player he is, proceeded to loot the coins from eyes of the zombies."

**Summary 8:**
> "Dallor Dallor also attempted to sell his various, lovingly cared for 'treasures' with mixed success."

The DM notes describe Dallor as a rogue who "cut a deal with Mordred" (betrayal). The player notes show the other side: Dallor is a relentless opportunist who loots zombie eyes, steals candlesticks, and calls them "treasures." He is not evil -- he is survival-minded to the point of absurdity.

**For the pilot**: Panel 31c already captures this ("clever hands and too many pockets"). But add: in the tavern aftermath (Panel 45), Dallor should be pocketing something from a dead kobold while everyone else catches their breath. This is who he IS.

### 2.4 Stalwart's Physical Dominance as Comedy

**Player Version (Summary 0-1):**
> "Stalwart kicked off Strom/Grom and used their bodies as springboard into Jorgon"

**Summary 9:**
> "Stalwart Little in the usual fashion slams the wise guy into the city wall"

**Summary 8:**
> "Between Daina and Stalwart Little, poor Ace never stood a chance" (interrogation scene)

The DM notes treat Stalwart as a paladin of Bahamut with moral weight. The player notes reveal that Stalwart's players lean HARD into physical comedy -- body-slamming NPCs, using half-ogres as springboards, intimidating children. The gap between his righteous purpose and his enthusiastic violence is the comedy.

**For the pilot**: Panel 28 (Stalwart walking through town, citizens staring) already captures the "commands every room by existing" quality. But the springboard move from the player notes is a visual that belongs in Episode 2's combat sequence.

### 2.5 Nyx's Owl and Its Repeated Deaths

**Summary 8:**
> The owl "died a quick and painless death, shrouded in glory and triumph. It died knowing it had done its duty and that Nyx was proud." (dripping sarcasm from the summary writer)

**Summary 9:**
> "Nyx's owl familiar (who should probably be named at some point)" -- suggesting the owl has died and been resummoned enough times that naming it feels futile.

Later the owl IS named Hedwig (Summary 5). The running joke is that the owl is Nyx's most expendable asset and her most loyal companion simultaneously.

**For the pilot**: Nyx's inn room (Panel 29) should include an owl perched somewhere in frame. Not highlighted, just present. This becomes a recurring visual signature.

### 2.6 The Daina Hit Counter

**Summary 7:**
> "*note the total Daina hitting Dallor count for this week is 5*"

The player notes track how many times Daina hits Dallor per session as a running stat. This is pure table energy -- the barbarian smacking the rogue is a beloved tradition.

**For the pilot**: Daina is not in the pilot party. But this dynamic (physical character who hits the sneaky character) should transfer to Stalwart/Dallor interactions.

### 2.7 Brigit's Backstory -- The Wolf Who Was Driven Out

From the Backstories Compiled document, Brigit's full backstory reveals:

- She is from a small village outside WindHaven, not near Argos
- Her shapeshifting powers manifested uncontrollably as a teenager -- "She would change unpredictably and uncontrollably; usually as a quite fearsome wolf"
- The villagers killed her parents for defending her: "Her parents stood up for her, and were killed in her stead"
- She lived feral in the woods for years, "lost a bit of touch with civilization (beast vs. non-beast)"
- She left presents for outcast children -- "A rare flower here, a perfectly carved toy sword"
- She was driven out a SECOND time after transforming to save children from bandits: "She woke up from her unconscious state human again after they left... a few villagers had also seen her transformation"
- Her driving forces: survival, acceptance, vengeance for her parents, understanding her powers

**The DM notes have NONE of this detail.** The pilot captures the wolf-to-woman transformation (Panels 23-24) but not the emotional weight behind it.

**CANON FOR PILOT**: Panels 23-26 (Brigit's vignette) should carry the backstory's emotional core. The forest edge (Panel 23) should feel like HOME to her, not a waypoint. The shift to human form (Panel 24) should feel like putting on a costume. The walk through town (Panel 25) should show her discomfort -- barefoot, scanning, "more animal than she lets on." The notice board moment (Panel 26) should be her choosing to walk toward people despite every instinct telling her not to. She joins the recruitment because "something in the land is wrong and she cannot walk away" -- this maps to her backstory of always protecting the vulnerable despite being punished for it.

### 2.8 How Combat Actually Felt

The DM notes describe combat mechanically: "the party dispatched the kobolds." The player notes describe how it FELT:

**Summary 2 (the ritual chamber fight):**
> "Just when things looked to be turning grim, Dallor Dallorbill decided to cut a deal with Mordred" -- the desperation of a betrayal mid-combat
> "The barbarian was obliterated by a powerful spell from the phylactery" -- Daina's death is described with impact, not mechanics
> "Stalwart who had decided to play dead instead of joining them" -- a paladin of justice playing possum is both cowardly and strategic

**Summary 9 (the wraith attack):**
> "the wraith sucks the life out of Nocturnus Wayne all but killing him in the process" -- the tactile horror of undead combat

**For the pilot**: Panel 44 (tavern fight) and Panel 68 (the betrayal) need this visceral quality. These are not clean anime battles. People get hurt. People make bad choices in the moment. The tavern fight should leave marks (established in the visual style notes: "Berserk's violence weight").

## Appendix: Canon Authority Rules

When DM and player sources conflict, apply these rules:

1. **What HAPPENS**: DM authority. The DM controls the world state.
2. **How it FEELS**: Player authority. Brigit's real-time notes capture emotional reality the DM summaries cannot.
3. **What is SAID**: Player quotes > DM paraphrases. The DM summarizes; the players remember exact words and running gags.
4. **How it LOOKS**: Merge both. The DM provides architectural detail (four waterfalls, gear-pedestal, 12 kobold tunnels). The players provide atmospheric detail (the Sistine-chapel ceiling, the eerie chanting, the Scooby-Doo vibe).
5. **Pilot adaptation overrides both** when it makes a deliberate creative choice (relocating to Conquis Abbey, "KNEEL" instead of "RUN LEFT," Jorgen as the primary betrayer instead of Finkle/Mordred).
