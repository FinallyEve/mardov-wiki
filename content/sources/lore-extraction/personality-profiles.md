## Brigit Wildfire

### Core Identity
The heart of the party -- a woman who channels the wildfire of renewal, who writes everything down because she refuses to let any moment be forgotten, and who will burn the world to save her friends.

### Personality Type
ENFJ (The Protagonist) -- Brigit is the emotional center of gravity. She organizes, she documents, she worries, she rallies. She leads not through authority but through sheer force of caring. Her player literally writes the session notes, and that obsessive documentation bleeds into Brigit's character: she is the one who remembers, who tracks the goals, who calls out when something doesn't add up.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Protect her found family -- the party is everything
- **Secondary**: Understand and master the wild forces inside her (the Stag, the Progenitor form, the zodiac connections)
- **Secret**: She is terrified that the fire inside her is not renewal but destruction -- that she is becoming something inhuman, and that her friends will stop recognizing her

### Fears
- **Surface**: Losing party members in battle (she tracks every close call)
- **Deep**: That the Progenitor form is the real her, and the freckled girl with the dog and the autumn tree was just the cocoon

### Coping Mechanisms
Brigit copes by doing. She writes notes, she makes plans, she skyrites warnings over every town they pass. When things go wrong she becomes a flurry of action -- healing, strategizing, shape-shifting. She processes emotion through transformation (literally -- she shifts into animals when overwhelmed). Her humor is dry, exasperated, and grounded: "Don't you dare give us another quest." She is the designated "mom friend" who hides her exhaustion behind competence.

### How They Enter a Room
Brigit walks in like she owns the conversation but not the room. She scans for threats and for friends, makes eye contact with each person she cares about, and positions herself where she can see the door. She stands with her weight slightly forward, ready to move. If she enters in Stag form, the room goes silent -- antlers fill the doorframe, and her eyes glow with ancient green fire.

### Idle Behavior
Takes notes. Always taking notes. Scribbles in a journal, sketches maps, mutters to herself about logistics. Will absently conjure a small flame in her palm and roll it between her fingers like a worry stone. Checks on party members one by one -- a hand on Sprocket's shoulder, a glance at Varis, a nod to Bergs.

### Under Pressure
Brigit becomes frighteningly focused. The warmth drops out of her voice. She stops writing notes and starts giving orders. Her wildfire magic flares -- literally, embers start trailing from her hair. At peak stress she shifts into her most dangerous forms: Lox (her beast form, dripping with fury) or the Progenitor (when she stops being Brigit entirely and becomes something ancient and terrifying). She has killed with rage in her heart and it scares her.

### Moral Compass
Brigit will always protect innocents -- she skyrites evacuation warnings over towns without hesitation. She will never abandon a party member. She struggles with vengeance vs. mercy: she can be consumed by rage (Lox's maw "dripping with saliva"), but she also chose not to kill when she could have. She frames her morality through the lens of renewal: fire that burns to let new growth emerge. She would burn a forest to save the people inside it.

### Growth Trajectory
Early Brigit (pre-chapter 86) was a young druid finding her footing, uncertain of her connection to the Stag. By the Reunion (86), she has become a battlefield commander, coordinating between scattered party members across a continent. Through the Shadowfell (100) and the time-fractures (119-125), she grappled with versions of herself that died or never existed. The revelation that she IS a Progenitor -- not just channeling one -- broke something fundamental in her self-image. By the final battle (136), Brigit is no longer the girl with the dog. She is a curator of the wilds, capable of pulling half the material plane into the feywilde, and the weight of that power sits on her like armor she never asked to wear.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Upright but not rigid. Shoulders back, chin slightly down -- watchful, not arrogant. When relaxed, she leans on things -- doorframes, staffs, Sprocket's head.
- **Hands**: Never still. Rolling fire between fingers, writing, gesturing while talking. Her hands tell the story before her mouth does.
- **Eyes**: Wide and green and alive. She makes direct eye contact that feels like a challenge and an invitation simultaneously. When angry, her irises flicker with firelight.
- **Transformation tells**: Before shifting, she closes her eyes and takes one deliberate breath. Her hair moves as if in wind that isn't there. Freckles glow faintly orange for a half-second before the change begins.
- **In Progenitor form**: All warmth vanishes. Movements become slower, more deliberate, almost predatory. She tilts her head like a bird of prey assessing distance. The cracks in her skin pulse with her heartbeat.
- **Combat**: Fluid, druidic. She moves like water around obstacles, then strikes like a crashing wave. In animal forms, she fights with genuine animal ferocity -- no human hesitation.

## Bergs (Barnabus Fireforge)

### Core Identity
A dwarf who became a god and never stopped being a dwarf -- he forges connections the way his bloodline forges metal, and he would rather make a friend than win a fight.

### Personality Type
ISFJ (The Defender) who evolves into ENFJ -- Bergs starts as the quiet, loyal protector who acts through his hands (forging, fighting, connecting to the myconid network). As the campaign progresses and he literally ascends to godhood, his sphere of care expands from "my people" to "all people," but he never loses the fundamental dwarven groundedness.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Protect the network -- first the myconid network, then the party, then ultimately all of Mardov
- **Secondary**: Forge something that matters, something that outlasts him
- **Secret**: He wants to be enough. Just enough. "I may be the newest god, but I am enough."

### Fears
- **Surface**: Losing people from the network (when connections drop, he panics)
- **Deep**: That becoming a god means becoming alien, separate, no longer one of them -- that divinity is loneliness with more power

### Coping Mechanisms
Bergs copes by working with his hands. When stressed, he forges. He tinkers. He reaches out through the network to touch other minds. He is the dwarf who says "does this guy need anything?" when chained to a wall next to enemies. His humor is understated and surprising -- "eldritch blasts from his beard" level absurdity delivered with a straight face.

### How They Enter a Room
Bergs fills a doorway. Not dramatically -- he's just wide. He pauses at the threshold, takes stock, nods once, and walks to whoever needs him most. After his ascension, there's a faint warmth that enters with him, and mushroom spores drift in his wake like tiny constellations.

### Idle Behavior
Forges. Always forging something -- a trinket, a repair, a modification. Checks the network like checking a phone: eyes unfocus briefly as he pings connected minds. Braids and unbraids sections of his beard. Sits with his back against something solid.

### Under Pressure
Bergs becomes the immovable object. His voice drops lower, his movements slow down, and he plants his feet. He doesn't panic; he anchors. In extreme moments, he connects to the full network and speaks with the weight of every mind linked to his. He is the one who says "there is no such thing as a lone wolf" when someone tries to sacrifice themselves alone.

### Moral Compass
Bergs tried to feed a mushroom spore to Tiamat's heads to redeem them rather than kill them. That tells you everything. He will always try diplomacy first, connection second, and violence last. He gave himself to the Stone Table to save everyone else. He would never sacrifice the unwilling, and he would always sacrifice himself. His moral line: no one gets abandoned, no one gets left behind, no one fights alone.

### Growth Trajectory
Bergs began as a mysterious, mostly-silent dwarven warrior with a wolf sigil. Through the campaign, he died and was resurrected by the myconid network, becoming something more than dwarf -- a living node in a fungal consciousness. He carried Kelemtor (a talking skull) as a partner. He discovered his twin-soul connection with Daina, learned he was heir to the First Forge, and ultimately sacrificed himself on the Stone Table to become the sovereign of the Feywilde -- a god of connection who is simultaneously the Demon Prince of Pleasure. He carries all of these contradictions with the same steady hands that always forged and fought.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Low center of gravity, wide stance. Moves like a boulder that decided to walk -- deliberate, unstoppable, but not aggressive.
- **Beard**: His beard is a character unto itself. It moves with his emotions: bristles when angry, relaxes when calm, braids tighten when focused. After ascension, faint luminescence pulses through the beard braids.
- **Hands**: Thick, scarred, always doing something. He touches things to understand them -- walls, weapons, faces. His grip is the gentlest thing about him.
- **Eyes**: Deep-set, watchful, warm. When he accesses the network, his irises briefly flash with bioluminescent blue-green (myconid light).
- **Post-ascension**: He moves the same but the air moves differently around him. Light bends slightly. Spores drift. He still acts like a dwarf, which makes the godhood feel startling by contrast.
- **Combat**: Economical, brutal, efficient. Every swing is a blacksmith's practiced strike. After ascension, his weapon Peacemaker hovers above him, radiating light.

## Diesa "Daina" Fireforge

### Core Identity
A woman forged in exile and hardened by loss, who carries a lineage she doesn't understand and a rage she channels into protecting everyone she couldn't protect before.

### Personality Type
ISTP (The Virtuoso) -- Daina is practical, action-oriented, and emotionally guarded. She shows love through doing, not saying. She is the person who just throws herself up a tower because the math says it's faster than the stairs.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Find and destroy the black-eyed, red-bearded stranger who murdered her father
- **Secondary**: Understand what she is -- the Fireforge name, the heirloom, the warforged that respond to her heartbeat
- **Secret**: She wants a home. A real one, not a cave in the Frozen Forest, not a wanderer's bedroll -- a place where the Fireforge name means something good again

### Fears
- **Surface**: Being too late (arriving after the damage is done, like she was for her father)
- **Deep**: That she IS her father's legacy of exile -- that the Fireforge name is cursed, not blessed, and everything she touches will be driven into the cold

### Coping Mechanisms
Daina copes with violence and motion. She rages (literally -- barbarian rage), she fights, she walks toward danger because standing still makes her think. She rereads old letters in quiet moments. She talks to the warforged, who don't judge and don't leave. When something hurts emotionally, she tightens her grip on her axe and changes the subject.

### How They Enter a Room
Daina enters like she's already assessed the exits. Chin up, shoulders squared, axe within reach. She does not introduce herself -- she waits to see who introduces themselves to her. In dwarven spaces, she straightens her spine and her father's heirloom catches the light, and the room reacts to the Fireforge name before she can control the narrative.

### Idle Behavior
Cleans and sharpens her axe. Studies the heirloom (turning it over, holding it up to light, trying to read symbols she doesn't know). Stands watch when no one asked her to. Talks to warforged if any are nearby -- she treats them like equals, possibly the only person who does.

### Under Pressure
Daina becomes a weapon. The rage kicks in -- her muscles swell, her eyes narrow, her breathing goes deep and rhythmic. She doesn't shout battle cries; she makes a sound between a growl and a prayer. After the Reborn transformation, her runes glow orange under pressure, and she becomes genuinely terrifying: a platinum-haired dwarf wreathed in geometric fire.

### Moral Compass
Daina operates on a code of survival and reciprocity. She protects those who've earned protection and hunts those who've earned hunting. She took the Heart of the Forge into her soul bag when it begged her to run -- she ran toward the danger instead. She would never betray kin. She would never let a child suffer what she suffered. She struggles with mercy for those who remind her of the stranger who killed her father.

### Growth Trajectory
Daina began as a loner -- a red-haired dwarf with a dead father, a mysterious axe, and a target. Through the campaign, she discovered her twin-soul connection with Bergs, learned she was heir to the First Forge and the Bronzebeard dynasty, and underwent a literal rebirth (Daina Reborn -- platinum hair, glowing runes, warforged commander). She went from seeking one man's death to defending a civilization. The moment she reached out to the warforged and her heart connected to six of them simultaneously was the moment Daina stopped being an avenger and became a leader.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Compact, coiled power. She stands like a loaded spring -- ready to explode in any direction. Feet planted wide, hands loose but near weapons.
- **Movement**: Quick for a dwarf. She moves in bursts -- still, then sudden explosive action. "Just throws herself up a tower" -- she launches, she leaps, she charges. No hesitation.
- **Face**: Weathered, fierce, beautiful in the way a well-made blade is beautiful. Her jaw clenches when she's thinking. She has her father's eyes but doesn't know it.
- **Reborn form**: The transformation is the key animation moment. Her red hair bleaches white from the roots outward. Runes carve themselves into her armor, pulsing like a heartbeat. Her eyes go from brown to burning amber-orange. She doesn't look peaceful -- she looks like a forge at full heat.
- **With warforged**: Her body language completely changes. She softens. She touches them the way a handler touches a working dog -- with authority and genuine affection. Her heartbeat literally syncs with theirs, visible as a shared orange pulse.

## Varis Laindon

### Core Identity
A soldier who fights because he couldn't save his father, protects because he couldn't protect his village, and keeps watch because if he stops watching, it happens again.

### Personality Type
ISTJ (The Logistician) with a buried INFJ core -- Varis presents as the practical, duty-bound ranger-soldier. He organizes defenses, commands guards, makes tactical assessments. But beneath the military bearing is someone who watches sunrises alone, who carries a pseudo-dragon skull as a trophy-turned-talisman, and who will dump diamonds on a dying stranger to resurrect them without being asked.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Protect the weak from those with power and without conscience
- **Secondary**: Find Yeldan -- or at least find the truth of what happened to him
- **Secret**: He wants to believe his father is alive. He stopped expecting it, but he never stopped hoping, and that gap between expectation and hope is where all his pain lives

### Fears
- **Surface**: The return of Ned'zailok (a lich you can never be certain is dead)
- **Deep**: That he will fail at the critical moment the way the local lord failed Tower Bridge -- that when it matters most, he will be too slow, too cautious, or too political

### Coping Mechanisms
Varis copes with vigilance. He watches. He patrols. He takes the dawn watch so he can see the sun rise over the mountains and feel, for one moment, that the world is not ending. He deflects emotional conversations with tactical assessments. He cleans his weapons with obsessive precision. When pushed to emotional breaking point, he acts -- not with words, but with arrows. He once did 200+ damage in a single turn because talking wasn't going to save anyone.

### How They Enter a Room
Varis enters last. He holds the door for others, scans the room over their shoulders, identifies exits and threats, then positions himself with sightlines to both the entrance and the windows. He stands against walls. He never sits with his back to a door. If he trusts everyone in the room, he allows himself to lean against something -- that lean is the highest compliment he gives.

### Idle Behavior
Checks his bowstring. Sharpens arrowheads. Watches the horizon. Watches sunrises. He keeps a mental count of everyone in his vicinity and notices instantly when someone is missing. He doesn't fidget -- he is profoundly still in a way that reads as either peaceful or predatory depending on context.

### Under Pressure
Varis becomes terrifyingly competent. His voice goes flat, his movements become economical, and he starts calling targets. He radiates command authority that he normally keeps suppressed. In the highest-stakes moments, he channels the Knight of the Dusken Wood -- silver-haired, battle-hardened, a figure out of legend who fires arrows that glow with cyan light. He saved Nocturnus from death by crumbling a diamond to ash. He brought Brigit back when she didn't remember her own friends.

### Moral Compass
Varis hates petty politics with a visceral, personal fury (the lord who wouldn't act to save Tower Bridge). He will always fight for those "screwed over by petty politics." He would never abandon a position while civilians are still evacuating. He has a complicated relationship with authority: he respects the STB's Dockson because Dockson earned respect by threatening a lord to do the right thing. He will break rules for righteousness but never for convenience.

### Growth Trajectory
Varis began as a grieving son in soldier's clothing -- competent, dutiful, quietly devastated. Through the campaign, he was forced to confront his father's loss again and again (the Shadowfell, the time-fractures, the lich's ongoing threat). He grew from a ranger following orders to a commander giving them -- organizing city guards, inspiring former-drab to take up arms, declaring Silence in Cloud City with the authority of the Dusken Wood. The moment he scattered diamonds across a dying comrade without hesitation was the moment Varis stopped being a soldier and became a protector. By chapter 136, he is the archer who fires the shot that reverberates through a god's body -- 200+ damage, the culmination of every sunrise he watched, every watch he kept, every time he refused to look away.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Military bearing, but natural -- not parade-ground stiff. He has a ranger's easy readiness, always balanced, always able to draw in one motion.
- **Eyes**: The most important feature. He watches everything. His eyes move constantly, tracking, assessing, counting. When he locks onto a target, his eyes stop moving entirely -- and that stillness is more threatening than any war cry.
- **The Draw**: His signature animation. When Varis pulls his bow, everything slows. His breathing steadies, his pupils contract, his body becomes perfectly aligned. The cyan glow of his magical arrows reflects in his eyes. Release. The arrow flies true.
- **Emotion**: Varis shows emotion through small gestures -- a hand on a shoulder, a nod of recognition, the way he angles his body to shield someone smaller. He cries alone at sunrise. He never cries in front of others.
- **Knight of the Dusken Wood form**: Silver-white hair, weathered face, misty forest clinging to him like a cloak. He moves like fog between trees -- you see him and then you don't, and then there's an arrow where you were standing.

## Sprocket Overspark

### Core Identity
A gnome who hides world-ending power behind a wide grin and mismatched boots, who uses humor to deflect from the fact that he is carrying the weight of fate itself -- Sabine Ra's chosen vessel.

### Personality Type
ENTP (The Debater/Inventor) -- Sprocket is the mad scientist with a heart. He argues, he tinkers, he experiments, he pushes boundaries. But beneath the chaos is someone who understands cosmic-level magic and uses pocket sand and simulacrums with equal enthusiasm. He is the party's Swiss army knife: artificer, wizard, strategist, comic relief, and -- when it matters -- the vessel of the goddess of fate.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Understand everything -- every mechanism, every spell, every cosmic secret
- **Secondary**: Keep his friends alive using increasingly improbable gnomish ingenuity
- **Secret**: He is terrified of Sabine Ra's cloak and what it means that it chose him. He jokes about it because the alternative is screaming

### Fears
- **Surface**: Running out of spell slots at the worst possible moment
- **Deep**: That he is not Sprocket Overspark at all but merely a vessel -- that the goddess of fate is wearing him like a suit and the real Sprocket ended the moment the cloak settled on his shoulders

### Coping Mechanisms
Sprocket copes with invention and humor. He builds things: simulacrums, portals, turrets, snowmen with ruby eyes and beard hair. He cracks jokes in combat. He uses pocket sand as a legitimate tactical option. He names his servants after Nocturnus in frilly maid outfits. When genuinely frightened, he goes quiet and starts calculating -- and a quiet Sprocket is the most dangerous thing in the room.

### How They Enter a Room
Sprocket wobbles in like a toddler on stairs that are too big for him, then immediately says something that makes everyone forget he's three feet tall. He looks UP at everything with unbridled curiosity. He touches things he shouldn't. He asks questions before saying hello. If there's a mechanism in the room, he has already disassembled it mentally.

### Idle Behavior
Tinkers with whatever is nearest. Adjusts goggles. Talks to Scamp (his mechanical fox). Makes lists of things to build. Eats things he finds. Wobbles down too-large stairs. Creates minor magical effects for no reason other than to see what happens.

### Under Pressure
Sprocket becomes the smartest person in the room and stops pretending otherwise. The grin stays but the eyes go sharp. He starts casting from a place of pure mathematical precision -- Disintegrate to the center of mass, Hypnotic Pattern on the exact heads that matter, portent dice deployed at the moment of maximum impact. He once told a party member "you're not welcome here" while disintegrating a titan and it was the most serious he'd been in a hundred sessions.

### Moral Compass
Sprocket gave a beggar genie two gold coins "to improve your life" and was rewarded with a dragon ally. That's his morality: casual, instinctive generosity wrapped in chaotic behavior. He would never experiment on the unwilling. He struggles with the Tiberius parallel (making the same mistake as a man who was punished with immortality). He believes knowledge should be free and power should be earned, and he is uncomfortable with how much of his power was given rather than earned.

### Growth Trajectory
Sprocket began as a colorful gnome inventor -- chaotic, brilliant, funny. Through the campaign, he became the vessel for Sabine Ra, gained the cloak of the Raven Queen, learned to channel necromancy, and was entrusted with the Anchor of Death. He went from "gnome with gadgets" to "the person who stands between the living and the dead." The moment Anemone couldn't hold on and Sprocket realized there wouldn't be another alabaster seat because he didn't die was the moment the joke stopped being funny. By chapter 136, Sprocket is the gnome who makes Nocturnous maid outfits AND the living conduit of cosmic fate, and he holds both truths simultaneously because the alternative is letting go of one of them.

### Animation Notes
- **Size**: ALWAYS animate Sprocket at gnome scale relative to the world. Everything is too big for him. Stairs. Chairs. Doorknobs. He has solved each of these problems with a different ridiculous invention.
- **Movement**: Bouncy, erratic, fast. He moves like a pinball between points of interest. When running, his hat bobs, his pouches jingle, his goggles bounce on his forehead.
- **Grin**: The default expression. Wide, genuine, slightly unhinged. The grin only drops in two circumstances: when someone he loves is in danger, and when he's calculating something that will change the outcome of a battle.
- **Wings**: His crystalline mechanical wings are expressive -- they flutter when excited, fold tight when scared, spread wide when he's about to do something dramatic.
- **The Cloak**: When Sprocket channels Sabine Ra, the cloak manifests as dark feathered shadows that spread behind him like raven wings. His eyes go deep purple-black. He grows a few inches taller. The grin is replaced by something ancient and knowing. Then it's over, and he wobbles on the too-big stairs again.

## Nyx Khamestria

### Core Identity
The unwanted twin -- a girl who was sold before she was born, rejected by the buyer and the seller alike, who found power in being the one nobody planned for.

### Personality Type
INFP (The Mediator) -- Nyx is idealistic, deeply feeling, and perpetually at war with a world that told her she was a mistake. She processes everything internally. Her melancholy is not performative; it's the natural state of someone who learned as a child that her parents considered her a "failed experiment." Her idealism survived that -- and that survival is her greatest strength.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Save her sister Xyn from being used as a vessel by Reynardine
- **Secondary**: Prove that being the "wrong twin" doesn't make her wrong -- that the powers she was never meant to have are hers now
- **Secret**: She wants her parents to see what she's become and regret everything. She would never say this. She barely lets herself think it.

### Fears
- **Surface**: Losing control of her warlock powers (they were never meant for her)
- **Deep**: That Reynardine was right to reject her -- that Xyn really is the better version of her, and that Nyx's existence is just an error in a cosmic ledger

### Coping Mechanisms
Nyx copes with withdrawal and reinvention. She crosses her arms. She watches from the edges. She created the Knight of the Flower Field persona -- a cheerful, whimsical witch girl on a broomstick with an owl -- as a direct counter to everything dark about her origin. The Flower Field is the life she would have lived if she'd been loved. She also copes with knowledge: she studies, she researches, she tries to understand the fey pact that owns her soul because understanding is the first step to control.

### How They Enter a Room
Nyx enters rooms like she's apologizing for existing, then immediately resents that she does. Shoulders slightly hunched, arms crossed, chin down -- the posture of someone who learned to be small. But her eyes are watchful, intelligent, and slightly defiant. She takes up more emotional space than physical space. In Knight of the Flower Field form, she enters on a broomstick with an owl, laughing, and the contrast is heartbreaking.

### Idle Behavior
Reads. Writes in a spellbook. Fidgets with her horns (she resents them -- they mark her as tiefling, as "demon child"). Adjusts her ribbon tie. Talks to her owl. Stares into middle distance, processing. She is the person at the party standing near the wall with a drink she hasn't touched, watching everyone else be happy and feeling both envious and protective of their joy.

### Under Pressure
Nyx's defiance surfaces. The shy posture straightens. Her warlock powers manifest as fey energy that crackles along her horns and tail. She channels raw arcana with the precision of someone who learned magic not through study but through survival. Her voice drops and becomes eerily calm. She is most dangerous when she is most still.

### Moral Compass
Nyx would never do to anyone what was done to her. She would never sell a child, never reject someone for failing to be useful, never treat a person as a "failed experiment." She extends empathy to monsters because she knows what it feels like to be called one. She struggles with Reynardine -- her patron, her abuser, the being who holds her sister -- because she needs his power to save Xyn, and accepting his power feels like proving her parents right.

### Growth Trajectory
Nyx began as a scared tiefling girl with powers she couldn't explain and parents who threw her away. Through the campaign, she discovered the truth: twin souls, the archfey Reynardine, the legacy of Tygras, and the fact that she -- the "wrong" twin -- was actually the one with the agency to choose. The Knight of the Flower Field was her first act of self-creation, and it mattered precisely because no one told her to do it. By the endgame, Nyx faces the choice of channeling pure fae magic at the cost of mastering and enslaving her patron -- becoming what was done to her in order to undo what was done to her sister. That paradox is the fire she walks through.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Closed, protective. Arms crossed, shoulders inward. She makes herself small. But when she chooses to stand tall, the transformation is powerful -- like a flower opening.
- **Hair**: Her voluminous purple curls are a visual signature. They bounce, they catch light, they frame her face. When she channels magic, individual curls lift and separate, floating on fey energy.
- **Horns**: She touches them unconsciously, a self-conscious gesture. When angry, they glow faintly. She sometimes tries to cover them with her hair.
- **Tail**: Expressive despite her attempts to control it. It curls when she's scared, lashes when she's angry, droops when she's sad. It betrays emotions her face tries to hide.
- **Knight of the Flower Field**: The complete visual opposite -- bright, open, laughing. Animate this form with maximum joy: windswept hair, owl swooping alongside, broomstick dipping and weaving. The contrast between this and her real posture is the character.
- **Magic**: Fey warlock magic should look involuntary -- like it's leaking out of her rather than being cast. Purple-green light bleeds from her eyes, her fingertips, the cracks in her composure.

## Nocturnus Wayne

### Core Identity
A nobleman who became a monster and is trying to use the monster's power for something noble -- a vampire knight who kills gods because someone has to, and because the darkness in him is already paid for.

### Personality Type
INTJ (The Architect) -- Nocturnus is strategic, brooding, and relentlessly goal-oriented. He operates alone by preference and in the dark by necessity. He has the emotional range of a gothic cathedral: vast, echoing, mostly in shadow, with occasional shafts of light that are more striking for their rarity.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Fulfill the prophecy -- kill the gods that need killing, bring order to the dark
- **Secondary**: Master the vampire within rather than be mastered by it
- **Secret**: He wants to be human again. Not the power, not the immortality -- just the warmth. He misses warmth.

### Fears
- **Surface**: Losing control of the hunger
- **Deep**: That Bane (his sentient sword/companion) is more real than he is -- that Nocturnus died the night he was turned and what walks around is just the sword's puppet

### Coping Mechanisms
Nocturnus copes with solitude, sharpening his sword, and very dry humor delivered at the worst possible moments. He sneaks into people's rooms to throw pocket sand at them. He chose not to win a battle to instead clear a path for others. He processes emotion through combat: every swing of his blade is a conversation he refuses to have with words. When overwhelmed, he goes transparent (Empty Body) and becomes literally untouchable.

### How They Enter a Room
Nocturnus doesn't enter rooms. He is already there when you notice him. He stands in the darkest corner, pale as death, long white hair catching whatever dim light exists. His red eyes find you before yours find him. When he chooses to be noticed, he steps forward and the temperature drops. He never knocks.

### Idle Behavior
Sharpens his sword. Stares at his own hands. Tests the edges of his vampiric abilities in small, controlled ways. Sneaks up on party members as "practice." Writes in a private journal that no one has ever seen. Stands in moonlight the way Varis stands in sunlight -- seeking something the night sky can't quite give him.

### Under Pressure
Nocturnus becomes the thing that monsters are afraid of. His pale skin goes translucent, his movements blur, his blade drinks blood in a "dusty, molasses pull." He fights with his girlfriend/sword Bane in a partnership that is more intimate than any living relationship he has. When he died and Varis brought him back, the world went monochrome -- and when color returned, his eyes had changed. He uses Empty Body to become untouchable, and in that form he looks young, like the man he was before the turning.

### Moral Compass
Nocturnus follows a code that exists entirely in his own head. He chose not to win when winning would cost more than losing. He cleared the path for others at his own expense. He has a prophecy that tells him to kill his aunt (a vampire queen sworn to Tiamat) and he will do it because the prophecy requires it, not because he wants to. He will never turn another person. He will never feed on the unwilling. He struggles with the fact that Lolth tasked him with killing gods and he's gotten disturbingly good at it.

### Growth Trajectory
Nocturnus began as a mortal warrior turned vampire -- brooding, dangerous, resentful of his transformation. Through the campaign, he absorbed a fragment of Asmodeus's essence ("I FEEL LIKE A GOD"), gained the darkest knight title, and led an army of shadows. The moment he chose to clear the pathway instead of fighting was his evolution from antihero to hero. When Varis used a diamond to bring him back from death, and the world was briefly monochrome, and then Nocturnus breathed again -- "not warm, not pleasant, quite a rude awakening" -- that was the character in one sentence. By chapter 136, he has a crystalline heart and the power of a dead god, and the party's response was "Nocturnous, you good bro?" and his answer was "I FEEL LIKE A GOD." He is the party member who scares everyone and makes everyone laugh, often simultaneously.

### Animation Notes
- **Palette**: Cool blues, greys, whites, blacks. The only warm color on Nocturnus is blood. Ever.
- **Movement**: Inhuman fluidity. He doesn't walk, he glides. He doesn't turn, he pivots. His hair moves half a second behind his head, like it's underwater. His blade is an extension of his arm.
- **Eyes**: Red pinpoints in sunken sockets. They track movement the way a predator's do. When he absorbs godly essence, they flare like suns.
- **The Sword (Bane)**: Animate the sword as a partner, not a tool. It pulses, it hums, it pulls toward targets. Blood flows UP the blade rather than dripping down. When Nocturnus talks to Bane, his hand relaxes on the hilt in a way that looks like holding hands.
- **Empty Body**: His body becomes translucent, glowing faintly from within. The Drow form emerges -- younger, almost beautiful, heartbreakingly close to what he was before. This form should feel ghostly and tragic simultaneously.
- **Gothic details**: Thorns grow from his armor. His cloak moves wrong (against the wind, or when there is no wind). Steam rises from his skin in sunlight. He casts no reflection.

<a name="stalwart"></a>

## Stalwart Little Goldentooth

### Core Identity
A paladin who worships retribution with a capital R -- not because he loves punishment but because someone once needed to be punished and no one stepped up, so he became the kind of person who always steps up.

### Personality Type
ESTJ (The Executive) with a deep ISFP wound -- Stalwart presents as the classic paladin: righteous, organized, certain. But his obsession with retribution masks a person who has been deeply hurt by injustice and is terrified of being powerless again. His faith is genuine but his anger is louder.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Deliver divine retribution to those who deserve it -- justly, completely, without mercy
- **Secondary**: Prove that faith without a deity is not weakness but the purest form of belief
- **Secret**: He is looking for Stewart, his lost friend, and the search has become less about finding Stewart and more about proving to himself that loyalty means something in a world that keeps taking people away

### Fears
- **Surface**: Being seen as small (his name is "Little" and he hates it)
- **Deep**: That retribution and justice are not the same thing, and that he has been worshipping the wrong one

### Coping Mechanisms
Stalwart copes with rigid structure and righteous anger. He follows codes, he cleans his armor, he prays to concepts rather than gods. When shaken, he becomes more rigid, not less -- his certainty increases in direct proportion to his uncertainty. He went to the Shadowfell to find a dead friend, which tells you everything about how he handles grief: he walks into the underworld rather than sit with the loss.

### How They Enter a Room
Stalwart enters like a parade marshal. Armor gleaming, shield forward, posture impeccable. His tail sweeps behind him with unconscious pride. He places his shield against the nearest wall (always within reach) and stands at attention until someone tells him to relax. He never fully relaxes. He is the last one to take off his armor.

### Idle Behavior
Polishes armor and shield. Prays -- not kneeling, not with hands clasped, but standing with his eyes closed and his shield against his chest, like a soldier at a memorial. Practices formations by himself. Argues theology with anyone who will engage.

### Under Pressure
Stalwart stops arguing and starts swinging. His morningstar becomes a metronome of justice. He shouts battle commands that are half tactics and half sermon. His faith manifests as radiant energy that pulses from his shield in concentric waves. Under extreme pressure, he channels divine magic without a deity -- pure conviction made manifest -- and the light that comes from him is not warm but blinding.

### Moral Compass
Stalwart believes in cosmic justice -- that wrong must be answered, that evil must be punished, that the scales must balance. He struggles with mercy. He struggled with the party and thought "we're not his friends anymore" when they did something he considered unjust. His Shield of Retribution is both his weapon and his worldview: when struck, strike back. He is learning -- slowly, painfully -- that retribution and redemption can coexist.

### Growth Trajectory
Stalwart began as a righteous gold dragonborn paladin -- certain, shining, slightly annoying in his certainty. Through the campaign, his faith was tested repeatedly: his friend Stewart vanished, he was captured and chained in the Feywilde courts, he discovered that divine power could be channeled without a god. The revelation that the Shield of Retribution combined with the Heart of the Faith grants deity-free divine magic was the pivot point: Stalwart stopped needing a god and became his own source of justice. By the endgame, he "looks like he's seeing ghosts" and only "kind of remembers" fighting alongside the party -- the time fractures have left him uncertain of reality itself, and for a character built entirely on certainty, that uncertainty is the real transformation.

### Animation Notes
- **Scale**: He is big. Dragonborn big. But "Little" -- play with this contradiction. He is surprisingly graceful for his size.
- **Armor**: Gleaming silver. Always gleaming. He polishes it obsessively. Dents repair themselves with radiant light. The blue tabard moves like a flag.
- **Shield**: His shield is a character. It faces threats before Stalwart consciously does. When he channels retribution, the filigree patterns on the shield glow, then pulse, then burst with light.
- **Tail**: Unconsciously expressive. Sweeps proudly when he enters rooms. Lashes when angry. Goes rigid when he's about to fight. Droops when he's doubting himself (rare, and devastating to see).
- **Gold scales**: In normal light, they catch gold and warm tones. When channeling divine power, they become mirror-bright, reflecting the light outward. His scales ARE his faith, visually.
- **Combat**: Heavy, committed strikes. Every swing is a judgment. He doesn't dodge -- he blocks with the shield and then punishes the attacker. His fighting style is a sermon delivered in steel.

<a name="tiberius"></a>

## Tiberius D Rex

### Core Identity
An old man who has seen too much and still believes -- a seventy-year-old priest who wanders the world spreading a prophecy no one listens to, getting sidetracked by every interesting thing he passes, because the journey IS the penance.

### Personality Type
ENFP (The Campaigner) -- despite his age and the weight of his mission, Tiberius is fundamentally curious, warm, and distractible. He spent 50 years in a desert and came out wanting to see everything else. He is the party's grandfather: wise, rambling, occasionally saying something that changes the course of the entire campaign, then forgetting where he put his staff.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Fulfill the Bahamut prophecy ("When the sand seas stir, and the matrim pact fulfilled, BAHAMUT's visage will lead the way, the Lost Nexus again rebuilt")
- **Secondary**: See everything, talk to everyone, experience the world he spent 50 years watching through desert sand
- **Secret**: He was an asshole in his youth. Young Tiberius was responsible for the destruction of the water genasi kingdoms. The 50 years of penance were not just tribal obligation -- they were personal atonement, and he is terrified of the young man he used to be

### Fears
- **Surface**: Failing Bahamut's prophecy
- **Deep**: That the young Tiberius -- arrogant, destructive, golden-armored -- is still inside him, waiting, and that the old scales and the red scarf are just a costume over the same cruel dragonborn

### Coping Mechanisms
Tiberius copes with faith, stories, and the gentle art of never quite arriving at his destination. He "keeps getting sidetracked" because every sidetrack is another reason not to face the culmination of the prophecy and what it might demand. He vouches for people at temple gates. He gives sermons that nobody asked for. He tells long stories about the desert that seem pointless until you realize, three days later, that they contained the exact information you needed.

### How They Enter a Room
Tiberius enters with the unhurried patience of someone who has walked for seventy years. He fills doorways with his bulk -- massive bronze/green dragonborn, whisker tendrils swaying, the red scarf catching the light. He makes eye contact with the most frightened person in the room first and nods at them. He sits down before anyone invites him to.

### Idle Behavior
Tells stories. Eats. Adjusts his scarf. Falls asleep sitting up and wakes with perfect recall of everything said while he was "resting." Examines objects with the scholar's care of a 50-year desert clergy. Talks to other dragonborn (Stalwart) about faith with genuine interest and gentle disagreement.

### Under Pressure
Tiberius stops being the kindly old dragonborn and becomes the Bronze Curator. His voice drops to a rumble that vibrates in chests. He speaks in prophecy, in scripture, in the voice of 50 years of prayer. He exercised dominion over a primordial dragon through pure divine authority -- the closest thing the party witnessed to direct intervention from Bahamut. Under maximum pressure, the young Tiberius surfaces: golden, armored, ruthless, and the old man has to wrestle him back down.

### Moral Compass
Tiberius believes in kindness as a discipline, not an instinct. He was de-aged and "was an asshole" -- his kindness is earned, fought for, maintained through constant effort. He was punished with immortality "to teach him kindness," and he accepted the punishment. He vouches for the party at Conquis Abbey because trust must be given to be earned. He would never destroy another civilization, and he carries the ghost of the one he already destroyed as his moral compass: "I did this once. Never again."

### Growth Trajectory
Young Tiberius was a golden-armored dragonborn warrior who destroyed kingdoms. Old Tiberius is a wandering priest in a red scarf who feeds mushroom spores to dragons and gets sidetracked by interesting rocks. The 50 years of desert penance transformed him completely -- or so he believed. The campaign's time-fractures de-aged him and forced the party to meet Young Tiberius, and the revelation that the kind old man was once a terrible young man recontextualized everything. When Tiberius "said THIS IS MY HEART" about Narissa Sunspot the changeling, and it was enough to undo him, that was the vulnerability beneath the faith: he loves too quickly, trusts too deeply, and a lifetime of penance hasn't made him cautious, just kinder about his recklessness.

### Animation Notes
- **Age**: Animate the weight of 70 years. His movements are deliberate, not slow -- he is still powerful, but he chooses each motion carefully. His scales have lost their sheen and gained a patina like old bronze.
- **Whisker tendrils**: They sway gently, always, like sea grass. They droop when he's sad and lift when he's animated. They are his most expressive feature.
- **The scarf**: Red, worn, important. He adjusts it constantly. It is possibly the only thing he's kept from his young self.
- **Size contrast**: Old Tiberius is massive but stooped. Young Tiberius is slimmer but upright. When the old man draws himself to full height, the room should feel smaller.
- **Faith**: When he channels Bahamut, his scales shimmer with platinum light underneath the bronze-green. His whisker tendrils straighten and point upward. His eyes go fully gold. The prophecy carved itself into the air around him in draconic script.
- **Young Tiberius (flashback)**: Golden scales, white and blue armor, single horn, cold eyes. He moves with the swagger of someone who has never been wrong. The contrast with Old Tiberius should be unsettling -- same being, zero shared warmth.

## Tesca (Tezka Duskhallow)

### Core Identity
A street kid in a monk's body -- she learned discipline from the monastery but her soul was forged in the slums, and she will never fully trust any institution that claims to protect its own.

### Personality Type
ESTP (The Entrepreneur) -- Tesca is action-first, instinct-driven, and allergic to hypocrisy. She thinks with her body before her mind, and her body learned to fight, steal, and survive before it learned kata and meditation. She is charming in a rough, unpolished way -- the kind of person who picks your pocket while making you laugh.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Experience the world her mentor described -- see everything, fight everything, taste everything
- **Secondary**: Prove that a tiefling street urchin can be as worthy as any temple-raised acolyte
- **Secret**: She wants to know what happened to Elder Roku after he tried to save her parents. She wants to thank him. She has never thanked anyone.

### Fears
- **Surface**: Being caged again -- the monastery, a prison, any institution that can throw her out
- **Deep**: That her parents died because they were sloppy, and she inherited their sloppiness -- that she is fundamentally careless and will get someone killed through the same recklessness that got Tenzin and Kiera killed

### Coping Mechanisms
Tesca copes with movement and mischief. She steals things she doesn't need. She pulls pranks. She picks fights she can win to feel in control. She channels her "propensity towards light crime" as a pressure valve for deeper emotions she can't articulate. When something genuinely hurts, she trains -- hitting targets until her knuckles bleed, running forms until her muscles scream, replacing emotional pain with physical pain she can control.

### How They Enter a Room
Tesca enters rooms the way a cat enters rooms: silently, from an unexpected direction, already having assessed every person and object inside. She stands loose, balanced on the balls of her feet, one hip cocked -- the stance of someone who can move in any direction at any speed. She smiles like she knows something you don't.

### Idle Behavior
Stretches constantly -- deep, fluid monk stretches that look like dancing. Tosses small objects and catches them without looking. Steals small things from party members and puts them back before they notice (or doesn't). Braids and unbraids the tassel at the end of her hair. Eats with her hands. Sleeps with one eye open (she learned this on the streets and never unlearned it).

### Under Pressure
Tesca becomes pure movement. Her monk training takes over: she flows between targets like water, striking with hands and feet and horns and tail. She doesn't rage like Daina -- she enters a state of perfect, violent clarity. Her breathing stays controlled even as she shatters bones. She is most dangerous when she is silent, because Tesca is usually talking.

### Moral Compass
Tesca hates bullies. She was bullied for being tiefling, for being an orphan, for being "wrong" in every space she entered. She will always fight for the outcasts, the misfits, the people who got thrown out. She steals from those who have too much and gives to those who have too little (sometimes she also keeps some). She would never betray someone who shared their food with her -- the foundational moral currency of a child who went hungry.

### Growth Trajectory
Tesca began as a wanderer running from her last rejection (the monastery). She didn't know the Diadem of Dawn was connected to her parents' deaths or that Elder Roku's kindness was the reason she survived. Through the campaign, she discovered the Diadem is actually the Anchor of Famine -- an artifact that grants one wish at the cost of all spellcasting ability -- and that her parents died trying to find it. The discovery that her tragedy was connected to the campaign's central plot transformed her from someone running from her past to someone running toward the truth. Her destiny: to wield the Diadem/Anchor to destroy Tygras's fae machine, which means sacrificing her magical abilities permanently. A monk who fights with her body accepting that sacrifice carries a different weight than a wizard giving up spellbooks.

### Animation Notes
- **Body**: Athletic, toned, in constant motion. Animate her center of gravity low and forward, always ready to spring. Her muscles are defined but lean -- functional strength, not bulk.
- **Tail**: Her tail is her signature animation element. It whips for balance during kicks, wraps around objects when climbing, lashes behind her when walking with purpose. Unlike Nyx's tail (which betrays emotions), Tesca's tail is a tool she uses consciously and gracefully.
- **Hair**: The long lavender braid moves like a pendulum when she fights and hangs still when she's at rest. The tassel at the end clicks softly.
- **Horns**: Small, curved, worn like a crown. She never tries to hide them (unlike Nyx). She leans into tiefling-ness with defiance rather than shame.
- **Skin**: Purple-violet, lit from within in certain angles. When she strikes, the contact points glow briefly -- ki energy visible as faint violet light at her fists and feet.
- **Combat**: Fluid, continuous, beautiful. She fights like a dancer who learned violence -- every strike flows into the next. Kicks, punches, elbow strikes, headbutts, tail sweeps. She never stops moving.

<a name="riven-fen"></a>

## Riven Fen

### Core Identity
A con artist who conned a god -- a rich kid who never earned anything in his life until he earned a cosmic debt, and now he's the most interesting fraud in the world trying to become genuine before the bill comes due.

### Personality Type
ENTP (The Debater) -- Riven is silver-tongued, lazy, brilliant, and fundamentally dishonest with himself. He talks his way out of everything because he never developed the discipline to fight his way out. He is the person who convinces his classmates to do his homework, not because he's dumb but because he's assessed the cost-benefit analysis and decided effort isn't worth it. The Kingmaker's pact is the first thing in his life he can't talk his way out of.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Discover the "Wind's Secrets" and the Boon his great-grandfather abandoned
- **Secondary**: Become worthy of the power the Kingmaker gave him (he knows he isn't, yet)
- **Secret**: He wants to be the person he pretends to be. He pretends to be brave, talented, destined for greatness -- and somewhere under the pretense, he desperately wants it to be true

### Fears
- **Surface**: The Kingmaker calling in the debt before he's ready to pay
- **Deep**: That he is exactly what his parents think he is -- a dropout, a failure, a lesser copy of a greater-grandfather who was already a failure

### Coping Mechanisms
Riven copes with charm, deflection, and changing the subject. He picks up new skills (magic, swordsmanship, music) and drops them the moment they require sustained effort. He lies -- not maliciously but reflexively, because the truth is usually "I can't do this" and he can't say that out loud. When cornered, he makes grand promises. When the orc had him dead to rights, he promised anything to anything listening. That worked once. It won't work again.

### How They Enter a Room
Riven enters like he owns the room and is graciously allowing everyone else to use it. His posture says "nobleman's son." His clothes say "dropout." His eyes say "I'm already two moves ahead of this conversation." He's the first to introduce himself and the last to give a straight answer.

### Idle Behavior
Touches the broken panflute around his neck (compulsively, like a rosary). Attempts to play it (one note, always the same note -- the one the Astral Wind produced). Reads other people's books over their shoulders. Practices rapier forms badly, then gives up. Eavesdrops on everyone. Sleeps more than anyone else in the party.

### Under Pressure
Riven's blade fights for him -- literally, through "the depth of his aspirations" rather than skill. Under pressure, his eyes go wide, his charm drops, and the raw desperation underneath surfaces. The rusty rapier materializes and fights with a grace he doesn't possess, and watching it is uncanny: his body moves with perfect precision while his face shows pure terror. When the toll of the blade overwhelms him, he faints. The brave front and the panicked reality coexist in the same body during combat.

### Moral Compass
Riven's morality is aspirational -- he wants to be good more than he is good. He begged for his life and offered everything, including something he doesn't have. He manipulated classmates and conned his way through school. But he also chose to carry the panflute "as a reminder to try again." He has the morality of someone who knows right from wrong and consistently chooses wrong, then feels bad about it, then promises to do better, then does wrong again. His redemption arc depends on the gap between who he is and who he tells people he is slowly closing.

### Growth Trajectory
Riven began as a spoiled, lazy, charming failure -- the kind of person you like despite knowing better. The pact with the Kingmaker gave him power he didn't earn and a debt he can't understand. Through the campaign, each time the blade fights for him, he glimpses what he could be. Each time he faints from the toll, he faces what he actually is. The panflute's inscription changed from "Follow the Astral Wind to find your Boon" to "Seeker of the Wind's Secrets, Property of the Kingmaker" -- his identity literally rewritten by the entity that owns him. His growth is the story of a con artist learning that you can't con destiny, and that the only way to pay a cosmic debt is to become the person you've been pretending to be.

### Animation Notes
- **Posture**: Aristocratic slouch. He has nobleman's bearing but refuses to use it properly -- one shoulder higher than the other, weight on one foot, casually leaning.
- **Hands**: Quick, graceful, pickpocket-ready. He gestures while talking (big, theatrical gestures). He touches the panflute at his throat dozens of times per scene.
- **The Rapier**: The key animation challenge. When the rusty rapier materializes, it comes with wind -- the Astral Wind that answers his call. Riven's face shows fear while his body shows mastery. The disconnect between his expression and his movements is the visual signature of his character. The blade is rusty, pitted, ugly -- but it moves like it's alive.
- **The Panflute**: Broken, silent, worn on a cord. When he blows it, only one note comes out, and that note carries the Astral Wind. The inscription glows faintly when the Kingmaker is listening.
- **Fainting**: After intense combat, Riven's movements stutter, his eyes roll, and he collapses mid-strike. The rapier vanishes as he falls. This should be animated as exhaustion, not weakness -- the toll is physical, spiritual, aspirational.

A farmer who became a killer because love demanded it -- he slew his own wife and carries the weight in a pair of crossbows named after children he couldn't save.

- **Deep**: That Molly's voice in his head calling out "Ian" and "Lilly" will eventually fade, and when it does, he'll have nothing left to fight for

Clay copes with routine and preparation. He maintains his crossbows obsessively. He keeps the inn clean, the silver polished, the holy water stocked. He doesn't drink alcohol -- he's seen what letting your guard down costs. He tells the story of Molly's Revenge to anyone who asks, not because he wants to but because speaking it out loud keeps it from festering.

Clay enters rooms the way a soldier clears a building: quick visual sweep, check the corners, check the windows, check for exits. He positions himself near the door with his back to the wall. Clay becomes cold, efficient, and lethal. He fights like someone who has already accepted that he might die -- no fear, no hesitation, just mechanics. Twin crossbows firing in alternation, bolts inscribed with names, each shot placed with the precision of someone who has trained specifically for this moment for five years. He doesn't shout. He doesn't posture. He just shoots until the things stop moving.

### Moral Compass
Clay killed his wife. He would do it again. That certainty is both his strength and his damnation. He believes in protecting the living from the dead, period. He will never show mercy to the undead (they are his wife's face wearing hunger). He treats children with a gentleness that breaks his own heart. He named his weapons after two children whose real names he never learned, and that haunts him more than Molly's death.

### Growth Trajectory
Clay's arc is the quietest in the party. He doesn't ascend to godhood or discover ancient legacies. He is a man who lost everything, built something from the wreckage, and keeps going. His growth is in the small things: trusting party members enough to sleep in the same room, learning that not all undead are evil (Nocturnus, Kelemtor), confronting the Alpha Vampire that escaped. His tragedy is that he already had his character-defining moment (killing Molly) before the campaign started, and everything since is aftermath.

- **The Crossbows**: Ian and Lilly are always visible, always within reach. He draws them in a single fluid motion that speaks of thousands of hours of practice. The inscriptions catch light.
- **Movement**: Economical. No wasted motion. He walks like someone who has learned that running draws attention and standing still draws vampires.
- **Hands**: Callused from farming, scarred from fighting. They shake very slightly when he's not holding a weapon -- he balls them into fists to hide it.
- **The Inn**: When at Molly's Revenge, his body language changes. He moves behind the bar with practiced ease, serves drinks with genuine hospitality. For those brief moments, he looks like the farmer he used to be. Then someone mentions vampires and the walls go back up.

He owes a debt to the air genasi (he failed to free Lapis the first time) and he returns to pay it.

### Growth Trajectory
Fen began as Darkeyes -- cloaked, hidden, face literally invisible. He was the party's stealth operative, their eyes in the dark. Through the campaign, his actions in Cloud City (the assassination, the failed rescue, the guilt) pushed him toward accountability. Taking the name BrightHallow was an act of self-remaking: the dark eyes chose to see light. His ascended form (Mehen becoming a spectral ice figure) represents the integration of his shadow and light selves. By the endgame, Fen has the Heart of the Faith and disappeared into the winter zenith alone -- the solitary figure completing his arc by doing the hardest thing in the light instead of the easiest thing in the dark.

### Animation Notes
- **Hood**: The hood is everything. When it's up, Fen has no face -- animate darkness where features should be, with only occasional glints of eyes. When the hood drops, the reveal should feel significant every time.
- **Movement**: Silent. He moves through spaces without disturbing them -- no creaking boards, no rustling fabric. Animate him as slightly out of sync with the environment, as if he exists half a step to the left of reality.
- **Mehen**: The fairy dragon is Fen's emotional externalization. Mehen is bright where Fen is dark, vocal where Fen is silent, warm where Fen is cold. Purple-lavender scales, pink iridescent wings, always in motion around Fen's stillness.
- **Mehen Ascended**: The fusion form is visually stunning: tall spectral figure, crystalline antler growths, flowing cyan energy, dark ornate robes. This should feel like a cocoon opening -- the hidden thing becoming the most visible thing in the scene.
- **Archery**: Fen draws and fires in a single motion that looks like a whisper. No wind-up, no dramatic pause. The arrow is simply there, and then it's somewhere else, and something has fallen.

<a name="knob"></a>

## Knob & Scamp

### Core Identity
An inventor and his best friend who happens to be made of gears -- Knob builds things because the world keeps breaking, and Scamp runs beside him because loyalty doesn't require a heartbeat.

### Personality Type
INTP (The Logician) -- Knob is the quiet problem-solver, the gnome who says little but sees everything. He approaches the world as a series of mechanisms to understand and improve. His bond with Scamp -- a clockwork fox he built himself -- reveals his deepest truth: he would rather build a friend than risk the vulnerability of finding one.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Understand how things work -- all things, from clockwork to cosmic
- **Secondary**: Protect Scamp (his creation, his companion, his proof that he can make something that lasts)
- **Secret**: He wants someone to build something for him, for once

### Fears
- **Surface**: Scamp breaking beyond repair
- **Deep**: That he is only as valuable as his inventions, and that without his tools and his fox, he is just a small gnome in a big world

### Coping Mechanisms
Building. Always building. When Knob is stressed, he dismantles something and rebuilds it better. Scamp is both his emotional support animal and his greatest creation, and talking to Scamp is how Knob processes everything. His lantern-staff is both light source and security blanket.

### How They Enter a Room
Knob enters rooms like he's casing them for parts. His eyes go immediately to the mechanisms: hinges, locks, pulleys, anything with moving pieces. Scamp enters separately, trotting in and scanning at floor level. They reconvene in a corner.

### Idle Behavior
Tinkers. Adjusts Scamp's gears. Holds his lantern-staff and watches the light play. Sketches blueprints on any available surface. Listens intently to conversations but rarely contributes unless asked directly.

### Under Pressure
Knob becomes a problem to solve, and he solves problems. His hands move faster than his mouth. He deploys gadgets, traps, inventions pulled from pouches in the right order at the right time. Scamp becomes his offensive arm -- the fox darting and biting while Knob creates the conditions for victory. Together they are a two-body tactical unit.

### Moral Compass
Knob values creation over destruction. He will always try to fix before he fights. He treats Scamp as a person, not a tool, and extends that courtesy to other constructed beings (warforged, golems). He would never dismantle something sentient.

### Growth Trajectory
Knob's growth is measured in Scamp's evolution -- as the fox gains capabilities and personality, Knob's own emotional range expands. He began solitary and ended interconnected, learning that the best mechanism is one that includes other people.

### Animation Notes
- **Scale**: Knob is small even by gnome standards. Everything towers over him. He compensates with confidence and a staff that is taller than he is.
- **Scamp**: The fox is gorgeous to animate -- golden-brown metal, visible gears, glowing blue core. It moves like a real fox but with mechanical precision: each step clicks, each turn is slightly sharper than organic. The blue energy core pulses with its "heartbeat."
- **Lantern**: The hanging lantern on Knob's staff is their campfire, their lighthouse, their signal. It swings gently when they walk and steadies when they stop. Its warm glow is the visual anchor of every Knob scene.
- **Hands**: Small, nimble, always occupied. Animate the speed of his work: screwdrivers spinning, gears clicking into place, blueprints unfolding.

<a name="birt"></a>

## Birt

### Core Identity
A bird who chose to be wise instead of free -- a powerful wizard wrapped in feathers, carrying humor as his shield and knowledge as his weapon.

### Personality Type
INTP (The Logician) with a comedic streak -- Birt is the party's high-level spellcaster who deflects the weight of his power with jokes (the "True Birt" Sesame Street image). He is smarter than he lets on and funnier than he needs to be.

### Motivations
- **Primary**: Master magic to its highest expression
- **Secondary**: Protect the phoenix (his unique responsibility in the party)
- **Secret**: He misses flying just to fly, without purpose or destination

### Fears
- **Surface**: Being caged (an aarakocra's fundamental nightmare)
- **Deep**: That his simulacrums are better versions of him and the original is becoming redundant

### Coping Mechanisms
Humor. Birt uses humor the way other wizards use shields. He blips in and out of existence (teleportation) in ways that are deliberately comedic. He blinds enemies and then makes puns about it. His white dove companion is both familiar and straight man.

### How They Enter a Room
Birt doesn't walk in -- he blips. One moment he's not there, the next he is, feathers slightly ruffled from teleportation, dove settling on his wing-hand. He looks around with a bird's head-tilting curiosity and speaks first, usually something unexpected.

### Under Pressure
Birt stops joking and starts casting. High-level wizard magic deployed with avian precision -- he sees the battlefield from above (literally, he can fly) and drops spells like a raptor drops on prey. He blinded Dallor the Titan and healed the phoenix through feather magic. Under maximum pressure, his plumage bristles, his eyes go sharp, and the comedy evaporates.

### Animation Notes
- **Movement**: Birdlike in every way. Head tilts, sharp eye movements, feathers ruffling. He walks with a slight waddle (taloned feet) but flies with absolute grace.
- **Teleportation**: His blip-in/blip-out should leave a small scatter of crimson feathers that dissolve.
- **Dove**: The white dove companion is always nearby, providing visual contrast to his crimson plumage. The dove is calm when Birt is excited and flutters when Birt is calm.
- **Casting**: His wing-hands weave spell components in the air -- talons tracing arcane geometries. Magical energy manifests as prismatic light refracted through his plumage.

## Kelemtor

### Core Identity
The recordkeeper of last words -- a skull who has been dead longer than most characters have been alive, who finds purpose in witnessing endings and remembering them.

### Personality Type

- **Last Words**: When someone speaks their final words near Kelemtor, his eye sockets flare, the words appear briefly as spectral text in the air around him, and then are absorbed into the bone. This is his sacred moment.

# Cross-Character Dynamics -- Voice Direction Notes

## The Core Emotional Axis
- **Brigit** is the heart (fire, renewal, documentation, caring)
- **Bergs** is the anchor (earth, connection, steadiness, forgiveness)
- **Varis** is the eyes (vigilance, duty, grief weaponized as protection)
- **Sprocket** is the brain (chaos, invention, fate itself wearing a gnome costume)
- **Nocturnus** is the shadow (darkness claimed and redeemed, the monster who fights monsters)

## Key Voice Dynamics
- Brigit and Sprocket banter constantly -- she is exasperated by his chaos, he is grounded by her organization. They are the comedy duo that pivots to devastating sincerity.
- Bergs speaks rarely but conclusively. When he says "there is no such thing as a lone wolf," the room shuts up.
- Varis and Nocturnus have an unspoken bond -- Varis brought Nocturnus back from death. They don't discuss it. They don't need to.
- Daina talks to warforged more naturally than to people. Her voice softens only for machines and for Bergs.
- Nyx and Tesca are both tieflings who handle their heritage in opposite ways -- Nyx hides it, Tesca weaponizes it. Their shared scenes should highlight this contrast.
- Tiberius tells stories that seem pointless until three sessions later when you realize he was giving you the answer the whole time.
- Kelemtor and Bergs are a buddy comedy about death and connection. The skull on the dwarf's belt, narrating everything, judging lovingly.
