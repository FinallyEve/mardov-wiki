Mardov World History

HUMANS:
1) Argos: (a port town by the shore ruled by an mayor who is elected every 2 years along with a few other key positions like Master of Commerce and Captain of the Guard)
2) Conq'is Abby: (a religious oligarchy, home to many races, the mecca of Mardov, founded as a sanctuary for a persecuted religion but has since thrived and now persecutes other religions)
3) Magister's Circle: (high seat of magicians in this world, a college to train the worlds magicians but will incorporate them into a rigid heirarchy, think wild magicians are dangerous to society and seek to collect wilders for conversion or worse)
4) DawnPass: (a crossroads town in the middle of the Dawnhaven continent, this is a place with a lot of foot traffic, think silk road meets border patrol, on the border between two major countries who have a long history of blood spilt between them)
5) Monethern: (technically a country on the other side of the world, this is ruled by humans and has a major port town also called Monethern (city-state) as well as multiple small farming towns along its main road, more rural than its western counterparts with the exception of the main city, not much love for dwarves in this country)
6) There is a society of pirate in the kraken sea, these people do not live in cities that can be on a map instead choosing to lash boats together and travel as a group of nomads on the high seas, they are the descendants of a people whose city is supposedly on the back of a giant turtle and it’s their people’s job to find it, they are called the Qual'xar

As a general rule of thumb, humans come from places that take in many different types of races but they are typically only RULED by humans, so while they accept others into their society and have representatives of all races, their leaders will always be human and there are varying degrees of tolerance from place to place (Dawnpass and the Qual'xar being the most tolerant (barring dwarves in Dawnpass and tieflings in Qual'xar waters)

GNOMES:
Gnomish are mostly rural farming villages, there is a great gnomish city atop one of the largest entrances to the underdark (Farthen' Har)

1) Farthen' Har: meaning great falls, this city is the last of its kind, an underground miniature metropolis within the luminescent mushrooms that grow along a giant waterfall, it is ruled by the Spore Keeper whose descendants declared martial law and founded Farthen' Har when all the ancient gnomish cities in the underdark were being systematically wiped out, it is from here that all modern gnomes call their ancestry

2) Sol'ville: the only truly village-sized village in a long line of farming villages created by gnomes who wished to live on the surface, these are the laid back, rural versions of their Farthen' Har cousins preferring to study science instead of follow stupid traditions, one of the true-blue democracies of Mardov.
3) Shroom-top: a neighborhood in WindHaven where the local gnomes are known for their mushroom based alcohol, outlandish magic spore farms, and lineage based government structure. Pacifists

ELVES:
Drow are all citizens of the Underdark, there are a number of Drow who chose to flee in recent years due to a rather hostile takeover of the royal line, the leader of such takeover recently taken to bouts of madness, these refugees end up in slums in most civilizations but oddly found refuge among the Farthen' Har Gnomes (who are no strangers to fleeing the under-dark)

Half-Elves are typically mildly accepted by both elves and humans, however they never truly fit into either social structure, too impure to rule either, the majority of the most powerful Half-Elves rule by merit rather than right, making them the ideal mercenary captain and one of the Fleet Commodores of the Qual'xar, there is a rather disproportionate number of half-elves living in Wind Haven as it is between the realms of men and elves and the number of brothels in that town keeps the world teeming with a steady supply of unwanted halfbreeds of all nature but mostly half-elves

Wood Elves: There are two last bastions of elvish culture left in the world, but if you ask any wood-elf they would say there was really only one left.

1) Eanvallan: the cultural head of all things elven, this is the last bastion that all elves can agree is the last remaining cradle of elvish civilization, a government structure that is entirely ruled by elders in a society whose people can live up to 750 years old, conservative is an understatement in this city. But that conservation has led to an interesting marriage of extreme environmental preservation and rigid social structure based on profession. With hundreds of years to perfect their craft the path to power is to head up each professions guild, who then is led by their divisions head which is elected by the current guild leaders, who is led by the council, five elders who serve for life and are only replaced by previous division heads. this city is a port but it is hidden among a vast forest
2) Machu: meaning city in the shadow, this city was originally founded beneath the Forest's Zenith and served as a the main cultural exchange between the almost Aztec society of ancient elves and the new influx of Artificer's Guild members in the temple floating above. This is a monarchy whose line of succession is bloody to say the least. This city is currently falling to ruin and has been for the last couple of centuries as a sickness known as "Shadow Madness" has darkened the hearts of even the most righteous of elves. while still inhabited by “wood elves" this city preferred to keep their foliage at the tops of great Aztec style pyramids as they lived between the roots below

HALFLINGS

Nobody has seen a Halfling Kingdom in over a thousand years, not since Ranar Surefoot angered the Tiefling god-king Tygras Fireblood and their entire civilization was wiped off the map. There are rumors of a great pilgrimage that resulted in the Halflings seeking refuge beneath the sand-dunes of the desert in south-eastern Dawnhaven as they fled from their Tiefling conquerors. The only way these ancient Halflings survived the trek was a pact known by the Qaul' xar (with whom the pact was entered) as The Terrapin Oath, the exact terms of the pact are passed down generation by generation by the subset of halflings who have an honored place as Oathkeepers among Qual' xar society.

Oathkeepers: lead by their commodore "Kraken of the Sand Seas" an honorary title passed on to the most brilliant Halfling captain in naval combat. On top of being gifted, the succession is marked by a circumnavigation of Mardov starting and ending with the "liberation" of a ship from Tygras Logar. Many Halflings find themselves in various port-towns opening various forms of farms, taverns, and pipeshops

TIEFLINGS:
Tieflings owe their absence of a large city entirely to their own lust for power. Tygras Fireblood is a name branded on the life of every Tielfing born in Mardov. The god-king thought he could conquer the world, enslaving the local halfling pacifists to build a fell-weapon the likes of which Mardov has never again seen. This weapon ran on a number of "anchors" which were housed in the various Tielfing temples around the globe. It wasn't until the unlikeliest of heroes, Ranar Surefoot interrupted, more like overloaded, the weapon's ritual by removing Tygras' own anchor at the height of its power which was aimed at the Qual' xar capital (still on the turtle at the time) wiping both the Qual' xar capital AND every. single. other anchor simultaneously imploded decimating the Tiefling civilization along with their enemies.

Now Tieflings are a marked race, called cursed by many. Their ancient capital city a crater in the shadow of the newly formed orc capital of Tygras Logar, Logar meaning spoils of war. As such, the Orcs of Mardov call Tieflings, Fel' Grandar meaning "the progenitors" because much or Orcish culture was built on the fallen Tygras empire (the ancient orcs of the area being treated as celebrities if they were able to work their way up the fighting pits).

DRAGONBORN:
Dragonborn, or the Curators as they call themselves, are sprinkled in the remote regions of Mardov in climates too extreme for the other races. The dragonborn civilizations are most notable for being true nomadic tribes, constantly migrating the frozen or desert wastes during seasons. Many of the seafaring tribes along the serpent sea use the Curators' great migration along the frozen forest as a way to mark the end of Winter and the beginning of summer. Along the desert of south eastern Dawnhaven the bronze Curators wonder the wastes in a form of exile for a covenant long broken. And lastly among the massive ring of mountains that makes up most of Bynar the red dragonborn have small villages and tribes all along the outer ring.

The governing bodies of these tribes is the Chieftain, for the Bronze Curators he is called by the honorary title "Malik ", for the Red Curators he is called "Eld", and for the Winter's Curators he’s is called "Nic".

On top of being a nomadic race, there are a number of dragonborn who's duty to their clan is to travel far and wide and learn/observe the other cultures of the world, these dragonborn are not called Curators, but instead are simply referred to as Children of Bahamut or Wandering Scales, depending on their personal beliefs.

DWARVES:
There are two great dwarven cities in Mardov, and one large collection of hill dwarves in the Frozen Forest.

Vag’ Mahar: This is the largest of the dwarven strongholds. It's so big that the city fortress is partly inside and partly outside in form of two large stone towers backed right up next to the mountain and a large stone wall tracing the cities outer districts. These dwarves are fabled craftspeople specializing in all manner of metals and gemstones. One of the most notable things about this city is it's three Thanes and how the three run their particular districts. Luckily the royal line of Bronzebeards keep the peace between the Thanes. The three thanes represent the 3 different colors of hair among the dwarven people: brown, red, and black. The Bronzebeards united the clans over 2000 years ago and Vag' Mahar was built partly in remembrance of their unification in the face of a grave and terrible threat from below.

Da' Gahar’: one of the dwarven rock kindergartens that served as an expressway for ancient dwarves to send raw materials back to Monethern via an elaborate system of minecart and tunnels. The Ways is known as one of the most dangerous places in the world due to cave ins, all manner of things that go bump in the dark, and its labyrinth of interconnected mineshafts are laid out purposefully so that only the Mole (the title of the head tunnel architect and union leader of the Explorer's Company) can decipher it. This is a title that is fought for due to extreme competition, only the smartest and most fit to lead may ascend to the title of Mole for a short decade long rule. Then the plans are scraped and a new Mole is chosen to be the successor from among the inhabitants

The hill dwarves of the Frozen Forest: these are some of the most remote tribes in Mardov, mostly because of the misnomer in their name. Despite the name "Frozen Forest" the majority of the land where the hill dwarves live is teeming with life. The fjords are a green angelic landscape for 8 months out of the year and a frozen wasteland the other 4. During that time the hill dwarves engage in an epic four month long feast filled with ale, last year’s harvest, and a ceremony to choose next year’s leader by way of taxes. The dwarf that brought in the most in taxes gets to set the plans for the next years planting. In that way, only the best ram ranchers, tuber distillers, and prospectors are allowed to plan and increase production for the next year.
