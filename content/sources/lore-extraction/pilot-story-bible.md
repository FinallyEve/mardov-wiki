# Mardov Pilot A -- Story Bible

**Series**: Heroes of Mardov
**Format**: Gothic anime motion webtoon (vertical scroll)
**Pilot Length**: 80 panels across 7 acts
**Visual Style**: Dark fantasy, manhwa ink lines, Illustrious XL 2.0 + gothic LoRA stack
**Aspect Ratio**: 832x1216 (2:3 webtoon tall)

---

## Logline

When a band of strangers answers a military summons to a besieged port town, they are led into an ancient dungeon beneath the streets -- and into a betrayal that reveals a sealed door bearing the crest of the Raven Queen, a god who should not have business with the living.

---

## Synopsis

On a windswept coastal cliff, Conquis Abbey receives an urgent military summons. Riders depart at dawn, traveling through a darkening landscape scarred by something predatory, and arrive at a port town bracing for a threat it cannot name. Within the town, a secret forge produces a weapon that burns with unnatural light -- a god-killer blade whose very existence hints at divine stakes the townspeople cannot comprehend.

As dusk settles, we meet the adventurers who will form our party: Sprocket, a gnome diviner reading fate in his cramped carriage study; Brigit, a druid who shifts from wolf to woman at the town walls; Stalwart, a golden-scaled dragonborn paladin who commands attention on every street; and Nyx, a tiefling warlock scribbling secrets in a candlelit inn room. Three more figures flash past in quick cuts -- a wild-eyed mage losing control of her own magic, a furiously short man elbowing through crowds, and a gnome with clever hands and too many pockets. All roads lead to the same recruitment yard.

There, a tyrannical gnome bureaucrat named Finkle -- three feet of immaculate suit and pure disdain -- takes command of the recruits with a triangle diagram placing himself firmly at the top. The awkward strangers spar, disperse, and reconvene at a tavern, where fragile camaraderie is shattered by the arrival of Jorgen, a bleeding ranger who crashes through the door with kobolds on his heels. After a vicious tavern brawl, a cryptic note draws the party underground through hidden sewer passages into architecture older than civilization itself. They face a kobold standoff in the dark, navigate trapped corridors, and survive a descending ceiling carved with a single word: KNEEL.

At the bottom of a spiral staircase, crimson light replaces torchlight. They enter a cathedral-scale ritual chamber dominated by four supernatural waterfalls -- blood-red, acid-green, mercury-orange, and soul-blue -- cascading into stone basins around a central altar. This is the pilot's full-page revelation: a space that should not exist beneath a port town, radiating power that predates human memory. Then Jorgen, the ranger who led them here, turns his weapon on the party. In the chaos that follows, they discover a massive sealed steel door bearing the crest of the Raven Queen -- concentric rings of forged metal inscribed in a language older than the common tongue. They stand before it, battered, betrayed, and confronted by a question none of them came here to answer: what lies behind the door, and why does a death goddess need it sealed?

## Act Structure

### Act 1: The Call (Panels 1-7)

**Setting**: Conquis Abbey, a crumbling gothic monastery on a coastal cliff.
**Tone**: Ancient weight, institutional weariness, gathering dread.
**Color Temperature**: Cool greys, storm-light, candlelit amber interiors.

The pilot opens with absolute silence and scale. Conquis Abbey is introduced as a place older than the conflict it now serves -- beautiful and broken, its flying buttresses cracked by salt wind, its gargoyles eroded into ghosts of their original forms. Ravens circle the bell tower. The sea churns below. This is not a place of adventure. This is a place that has seen too much.

Inside, the great hall has been repurposed as a war room. Maps and military correspondence cover every surface. Half-eaten meals suggest people have not left this room in days. A rain-soaked messenger arrives bearing a sealed scroll -- the wax seal is military, official, the kind that means mobilization. An officer reads the summons aloud to grim faces. The camera moves to the war table: a top-down map view with fresh red markers showing threat positions, and a single circled destination -- the port town.

Night falls. The abbey goes dark except for one amber window where someone is still deciding. This is the last quiet moment before movement begins.

### Act 2: The Journey (Panels 8-14)

**Setting**: The road from Conquis Abbey to the port town.
**Tone**: Cinematic travel, environmental storytelling, escalating unease.
**Color Temperature**: Pale dawn transitioning to dark forest greens and greys, ending in overcast harbor light.

Dawn breaks. Riders in scarlet cloaks depart through the abbey gates, silhouetted against pale sunrise. The road sequence is a landscape poem: winding through dark autumn forests, across old stone bridges over misty streams. The world is beautiful and wounded. An abandoned merchant cart with deep claw marks -- no bodies, which is worse than bodies -- tells the audience everything about what waits ahead without a word of dialogue.

The port town appears from a hillside: stone buildings around a natural harbor, smoke from chimneys, a handful of ships at anchor. It looks safe from a distance. At the gate, a queue of merchants and refugees tells a different story. Inside, citizens board windows, carry water barrels, and drill in courtyards. A notice board covered in recruitment posters and missing persons reports is the first direct storytelling panel -- the audience can read the town's fear in its own words.

### Act 3: The Forge (Panels 15-20)

**Setting**: A blacksmith's forge in a narrow cobblestone alley; port town at dusk.
**Tone**: Sacred craft, supernatural undercurrent, mythic weight.
**Color Temperature**: Intense orange forge-glow against dark stone, transitioning to golden dusk amber.

This is the first overtly supernatural sequence. In a narrow alley, a forge pours smoke and sparks. Inside, a smith hammers glowing metal on an anvil etched with arcane runes -- runes that should not be on a blacksmith's tool. The metal glows with colors that are not natural: blue-gold energy that suggests the blade being forged is not merely a weapon but something that answers to a higher authority.

The quenching scene is the act's centerpiece: when the blade hits water, the steam carries light -- crackling blue-gold energy along the edge. This is not metallurgy. This is ritual. The completed weapon rests on dark velvet, carrying a faint supernatural glow and arcane inscriptions that appear etched by light rather than by chisel.

The act climaxes with the smith raising the finished god-killer overhead, its light filling the entire forge. Then a quiet exhale: the town at dusk, golden and peaceful, unaware of what was just forged in its belly.

### Act 4: The Characters (Panels 21-44)

**Setting**: Various locations across the port town -- carriages, forest edges, docks, inn rooms, the recruitment yard, a tavern.
**Tone**: Intimate vignettes building to ensemble comedy, shattered by violence.
**Color Temperature**: Warm interior golds, cool exterior greens and greys, shifting to warm tavern amber.

This is the longest act and the emotional core of the pilot. Each main character receives a two-panel vignette that establishes who they are before they meet each other. The vignettes are carefully structured: an interior/private moment showing the character's true self, followed by a public/exterior moment showing how the world sees them.

**Sprocket** (Panels 21-22): A gnome diviner, ~3.5 feet tall, warm tan skin, golden-yellow spiked hair, large dark analytical eyes, gap-toothed grin, bushy sideburns, brass goggles. We first see him in a cramped moving carriage study surrounded by books and astronomical instruments, calculating star charts with feverish concentration -- his hands stained with ink, half-eaten sandwich forgotten. Then he arrives at the port town, small among the crowd, grinning with the expression of someone who knows something is about to happen.

**Brigit** (Panels 23-26): A young human woman druid. Dark auburn hair tangled with leaves and twigs, pale porcelain skin, green eyes. We meet her first as a grey-brown wolf at the edge of a scarred, recovering forest -- alert, poised, wild. Then the shift: mid-transformation from wolf to human at the town wall, liquid and organic, fur dissolving into freckled skin. She walks barefoot through town, scanning everything like a predator in an unfamiliar ecosystem. At the notice board, she touches the recruitment poster with recognition -- not mercenary calculation, but a druid sensing something wrong that she cannot leave alone.

**Stalwart** (Panels 27-28): A dragonborn paladin, seven feet tall, golden-yellow reptilian scales, spiky golden mane, dark amber eyes, heavy dark metallic armor with a scarlet surcoat. Dawn at the dock: sunrise catches his scales as he steps off a ship, and the dock workers stop to stare. Walking through town, he takes up most of the road. Citizens react with awe, wariness, stepping aside. He carries himself with weight and purpose -- a walking altar.

**Nyx** (Panels 29-30): A young tiefling warlock, petite build, pale pinkish-lavender skin, small curved horns, white/pale blonde voluminous curly hair. Candlelight in a modest inn room: she sits cross-legged on a bed, writing furiously in a leather journal, cobalt-blue cloak pooled around her. Then stepping through the inn doorway into morning light, pulling the cloak tight, journal tucked against her chest -- the posture of someone leaving something behind.

**Quick Cuts** (Panel 31a-c): Three vertical panels in rapid succession. A wild-eyed human woman in ill-fitting mage robes with uncontrolled magic sparking from her fingertips -- hot white-gold involuntary sparks, her expression equal parts terror and exhilaration. A 4-foot-tall adult human man (NOT a halfling -- adult proportions, just small) elbowing aggressively through a crowd, daggers at belt, eyes like a cornered predator. A gnome man with quick clever hands and darting calculating eyes browsing a market stall, his bag already slightly heavier than when he arrived.

**Convergence** (Panel 32): Bird's-eye aerial view of the port town. Eight tiny silhouette figures approach from different directions through the streets, all converging on one location. The visual metaphor is explicit: all roads lead here.

**The Recruitment Yard** (Panels 33-40): The ensemble assembles. A military training yard with scarlet banners, practice dummies, and one very small gnome named Finkle who makes up for his stature with absolute tyrannical authority. Finkle's entrance is a comedy set piece -- a door bangs open, and through it strides (minces) a tiny figure in an immaculate suit that still does not fit, trailed by two enormous half-ogre bodyguards. He commands from atop a wooden crate, presents a triangle diagram with FINKLE at the top and EVERYONE ELSE at the bottom, and introduces his bodyguards with the disdain of someone presenting unruly furniture. The recruits spar, size each other up, and are dismissed into the awkward groups that form when strangers are forced together.

**The Tavern** (Panels 41-44): Evening. Warm golden light from tavern windows. Inside, the fragile social dynamics begin to form -- Tiny Rick (the 4-foot human) and Dallor (the market gnome) share grudging respect at the far end of a table. Then Jorgen arrives: a ranger in a torn gold military tunic SLAMS through the door, gasping, bleeding, one hand on the doorframe -- and behind him, the night is full of moving shapes. The tavern erupts into combat against swarming kobolds. This is the pilot's first action sequence, and it is chaotic, messy, and real.

### Act 5: The Descent (Panels 45-61)

**Setting**: Tavern aftermath, then underground -- sewers, ancient corridors, trapped passages, spiral staircase.
**Tone**: Post-combat tension building to claustrophobic dread, punctuated by one moment of dragonborn authority.
**Color Temperature**: Warm amber (tavern) rapidly cooling to torchlit orange against black stone, ending in unnatural crimson.

The tavern is wrecked. Dead kobolds, spilled ale, a smoldering tapestry. In the aftermath, a folded note is discovered -- fussy precise handwriting, lit by dim amber light. The party huddles around the table with the note at center, a single candle lighting their faces from below. The decision is made.

Jorgen briefs them at the bar, then leads them to a hidden sewer entrance behind a nondescript building. The descent begins. The architecture changes as they go deeper: crude sewer walls give way to something older, precisely carved geometric patterns that predate the town by centuries. The air changes. The sounds change.

They encounter kobolds in the dark -- hundreds of reptilian eyes reflecting their single torch. A standoff crystallizes: neither side moves. Then Stalwart steps forward and speaks in Draconic, his voice carrying the authority of his lineage. The kobolds retreat, parting like a tide.

Deeper still. Trap corridors with visible pressure plates and suspicious grooves. Jorgen holds open a heavy stone door while the party rushes through. Then the ceiling begins to descend -- a massive stone slab dropping slowly, inexorably. The most claustrophobic panel in the pilot: a vertically compressed frame showing the ceiling terrifyingly close, and carved into its surface in dried blood, one word: KNEEL.

A spiral staircase descends into darkness. The party is strung out along the spiral, each a level apart, connected by torchlight but separated by stone. At the bottom, red light hits them -- not torchlight, not fire, something supernatural. Their faces go crimson. Their expressions shift from exhaustion to recognition that they have arrived somewhere that should not exist.

### Act 6: The Chamber (Panels 62-75)

**Setting**: The ritual chamber -- a cathedral-scale underground space with four supernatural waterfalls.
**Tone**: Revelation, betrayal, mythic confrontation, unanswered questions.
**Color Temperature**: Four-color supernatural palette: blood-red, acid-green, mercury-orange, soul-blue. Overwhelming, clashing, alive.

This is the pilot's climax and its reason for existing. Panel 64 -- the full-page ritual chamber reveal -- is the single most important image in the entire pilot.

Two stacked close-up portraits open the act: Jorgen's face shifts for one beat -- surprise, the calculation of a caught man, then a mask sliding back into place. The other face shows a party member registering what they just saw. Something is wrong with Jorgen. The seed of betrayal is planted in a glance.

The party steps through a massive stone archway, silhouetted in the entrance, and beyond them the four-colored light blazes. Then the full-page reveal: a cathedral-scale ritual chamber with a vaulted roof vanishing into darkness. Four supernatural waterfalls cascade from openings high in the walls into stone basins arranged around a central altar. Blood-red: thick, hot, luminous, the color of sacrifice. Acid-green: pulsing with organic corruption, the color of pestilence. Mercury-orange: metallic, refracting light like molten iron, the color of war. Soul-blue: translucent, shimmering, with half-formed faces appearing within, the color of death. At the center, the altar, and resting on it: the crest of the Raven Queen.

Sprocket's reaction close-up captures the gnome's analytical mind overwhelmed by something that exceeds calculation. Then Jorgen's mask slips completely -- standing at the threshold, his expression has changed entirely. He is not a rescuer. He led them here on purpose. He draws his weapon against the party.

In the chaos of betrayal and combat, the party discovers a massive ancient steel door on the far wall, sealed with concentric rings of forged metal. At its center: the crest of the Raven Queen -- a stylized raven with wings spread, talons gripping an ambiguous object. The surface inscriptions are in a language that predates the common tongue.

The party stands before the sealed door, weapons lowered, backlit by four-colored waterfall light. Each face shows a different reaction: determination, fear, fascination, fury. The question hangs in the air: what lies behind this door?

Wide final shot of the full chamber. Waterfalls framing the composition, the sealed door at center, the party small before it. ## Character Guide

### Player Characters (The Party)

**Sprocket** -- Gnome Diviner
- *Appearance*: ~3.5 ft tall. Warm tan skin. Golden-yellow spiked hair. Large dark analytical eyes. Gap-toothed grin with a sharp edge. Bushy sideburns. Brass goggles (usually on forehead).
- *Clothing*: Ornate academic robes, ink-stained. Pockets full of lenses and instruments.
- *Personality*: Feverishly intellectual. Sees the world as a system of patterns to decode. His calculation occasionally blindsides him with wonder -- the ritual chamber overwhelms his analytical framework.
- *Role in Pilot*: The eyes of the party. His reaction shots anchor the audience's understanding of supernatural events.
- *Panels*: 21, 22, 65, 69

**Brigit** -- Human Druid (Shapeshifter)
- *Appearance*: Young woman. Dark auburn hair tangled with leaves and twigs. Pale porcelain skin with freckles. Green eyes -- scanning, alert, predatory.
- *Clothing*: Practical earth tones. Barefoot. Looks like the forest did not fully let her go.
- *Personality*: Quiet, watchful, more animal than she lets on. She does not assess things as a person does; she reads ecosystems. Joins the recruitment because something in the land is wrong and she cannot walk away.
- *Wolf Form*: Grey-brown, lean, alert. The shift between forms is liquid and organic.
- *Panels*: 23, 24, 25, 26

**Stalwart** -- Dragonborn Paladin
- *Appearance*: Seven feet tall. Muscular warrior build. Golden-yellow reptilian scales. Spiky golden mane. Dark amber eyes. Sharp dragon-like facial features.
- *Clothing*: Heavy dark metallic armor with a scarlet surcoat. Greatsword on back.
- *Personality*: Commands every room by existing. The weight of duty and lineage is visible in how he carries himself. When he speaks Draconic to the kobolds, it is not a trick -- it is heritage asserting itself.
- *Role in Pilot*: The physical anchor. His size and authority provide the party's first real victory (the kobold standoff).
- *Panels*: 27, 28, 53, 54

**Nyx** -- Tiefling Warlock
- *Appearance*: Petite build. Pale pinkish-lavender skin. Small curved horns. White/pale blonde voluminous curly hair.
- *Clothing*: Cobalt-blue cloak. Leather journal always close.
- *Personality*: Private, intense, writing constantly. Her journal is her anchor. She leaves her inn room with the posture of someone leaving something behind -- her past, her safety, or both.
- *Panels*: 29, 30

**The Wild Mage** (unnamed in pilot) -- Human Mage
- *Appearance*: Young woman. Wild-eyed. Ill-fitting, mismatched mage robes.
- *Signature*: Uncontrolled white-gold magic sparking involuntarily from fingertips. Equal parts terror and exhilaration.
- *Panels*: 31a

**Tiny Rick** -- Human (Short Stature)
- *Appearance*: 4 feet tall, adult human proportions (NOT a halfling). Angular, wrong for his size. Daggers at belt.
- *Personality*: Aggressively compensating. Elbows through crowds. Eyes of a cornered predator who chose to attack first.
- *Panels*: 31b, 42

**Dallor** -- Gnome Rogue
- *Appearance*: Small stature. Quick clever hands. Darting calculating eyes. Practical traveling clothes with many pockets.
- *Personality*: Quiet, opportunistic, always slightly richer than when he arrived. Shares grudging mutual respect with Tiny Rick.
- *Panels*: 31c, 42

### NPCs

**Finkle** -- Gnome Bureaucrat/Drill Sergeant
- *Appearance*: Tiny gnome in an immaculate tailored suit that still does not fit. Flanked by two enormous half-ogre bodyguards.
- *Personality*: Pure disdain. Absolute authority. Stands on crates to achieve something approaching normal height. His triangle management diagram (FINKLE at top, EVERYONE ELSE at bottom) tells you everything.
- *Role in Pilot*: Comedy anchor and authority figure for the recruitment sequence.
- *Panels*: 35, 36, 37, 38

**Jorgen** -- Human Ranger (Betrayer)
- *Appearance*: Handsome, professional. Gold military tunic (torn and bloodied on arrival). Scratched face, composed bearing.
- *Personality*: Presents as a competent, wounded military professional. His mask is convincing. When it drops in the ritual chamber, the shift is total -- cold, purposeful, calculating. He led the party underground deliberately.
- *Role in Pilot*: The guide who becomes the threat. His betrayal in the ritual chamber is the pilot's dramatic pivot.
- *Panels*: 43, 48, 57, 62, 67, 68

**The Smith** -- Unnamed Blacksmith
- *Appearance*: Muscular, anonymous. Defined by his work, not his identity.
- *Role in Pilot*: The hands that forge the god-killer weapon. His forge sequence introduces supernatural elements before the party encounters them.
- *Panels*: 16, 17, 19

## Visual Style Notes

*Drawn from the pacing study and production design.*

### Overall Aesthetic
- **Gothic anime motion webtoon** -- not clean cel-shaded modern webtoon. Every surface has history: worn stone, scarred wood, weathered cloth.
- **Motivated light sources only**: torches, moonlight, candlelight, forge-glow, supernatural waterfall light. No generic illumination.
- **Vagabond-influenced backgrounds**: rough brush stroke energy, lived-in detail. The world existed before this story and will continue after.
- **Berserk's violence weight**: injuries matter. The tavern fight leaves marks. Jorgen's scratches are not decorative -- they establish his cover story.

### Color System
The pilot establishes the **four-waterfall color grammar** that will define the entire series:

| Waterfall | Color | Concept | Hex Range |
|-----------|-------|---------|-----------|
| First | Blood-red | Sacrifice | Deep crimson, hot, luminous |
| Second | Acid-green | Pestilence | Toxic, pulsing, organic |
| Third | Mercury-orange | War | Metallic, brilliant, aggressive |
| Fourth | Soul-blue | Death | Translucent, cold, spectral |

These four colors should appear ONLY in the ritual chamber and credits in the pilot. Their absence from the rest of the episode makes their appearance overwhelming. In future episodes, traces of these colors will bleed into the surface world as the sealed door's influence spreads.

### Camera Language
- **Establishing shots**: Wide, slightly elevated. Used for location introductions (abbey, town, chamber).
- **Character introductions**: Mid-shot or close-mid, intimate. Each character gets framing appropriate to their nature (Stalwart: low angle; Sprocket: interior/cozy; Brigit: forest/liminal).
- **Action sequences**: Alternate extreme close-up (impact/expression) with wide shots (consequence/geography).
- **The ritual chamber reveal**: Must use a FULL-PAGE panel. The scroll distance to traverse it should feel like entering the space.
- **Breath panels**: After the tavern fight and after the betrayal -- near-empty panels that let the audience land.

### Panel Density Rhythm
Following the pacing study's recommendation:
- Acts 1-2: Lower panel density. Wide panels. Let the world breathe.
- Act 3 (Forge): Tighter panels for the crafting sequence, one wide dramatic for the completion.
- Act 4 (Characters): Two-panel vignettes per character, wider for the yard, tighter for the tavern.
- Act 5 (Descent): Increasing density and compression as the space gets smaller. The KNEEL panel should feel physically oppressive.
- Act 6 (Chamber): The full-page reveal breaks all preceding density expectations. Then rapid-fire panels for the betrayal.
- Act 7 (Credits): Return to wide, graphic, breathing space.

## Thematic Through-Lines

### The Sealed Door
The central mystery of the pilot. A death goddess sealed something beneath this town, and the seal is ancient, deliberate, and inscribed in a language that predates human civilization. The door is not a barrier to treasure -- it is a warning. The pilot's question is not "what's inside?" but "why does the Raven Queen need something sealed?"

### The Four Waterfalls
Sacrifice (red), Pestilence (green), War (orange), Death (blue). These four concepts are the pillars of whatever ritual this chamber was built to contain. Their colors will become the visual DNA of the entire series. In the pilot, they appear ONLY in the underground chamber and the credits -- their power comes from their absence everywhere else.

### Strangers Becoming a Party
The pilot traces the oldest D&D story: strangers meet, are thrown together by circumstance, and discover they need each other. Finkle's recruitment yard is the mechanical assembly. The tavern is the first fragile social bond. The dungeon is where the bond is tested. The betrayal is where it hardens into something real.

### Jorgen's Betrayal
The ranger who rescues the party is the one who endangered them. This inverts the "quest-giver NPC" trope and teaches the audience that in Mardov, trust is earned, not granted by narrative role. Jorgen's mask is good enough to fool the party and the audience. When it drops, the pilot retroactively becomes a different story.
