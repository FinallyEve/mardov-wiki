# Mardov Campaign -- Music Direction Bible

## Philosophy

The Mardov score operates on two simultaneous layers:

1. **Dark Fantasy Orchestral Throughline** -- Non-diegetic. A modern cinematic orchestral score in the vein of Bear McCreary (God of War), Ramin Djawadi (Game of Thrones), and Yoko Shimomura (Kingdom Hearts). This is the backbone. It carries the narrative weight, underscores battles, and provides emotional architecture for all five acts.

2. **Character-Specific Genre Themes** -- Each PC and key NPC has a genre assignment that reflects their personality, arc, and soul. These themes weave into the orchestral throughline: sometimes surfacing as solo moments, sometimes blending beneath the full orchestra, sometimes clashing against it when a character is in conflict with their world.

### The Raven Queen Throughline

A **flute/piccolo motif** serves as the unifying thread across the entire score. This motif represents the Raven Queen, Sabine Ra, fate, and death -- the cosmic forces that bind every character together. It appears:

- As a **distant solo flute** in quiet moments (campfire scenes, travel montages)
- As a **piccolo trill woven into combat brass** during battle
- As a **full woodwind choir** during moments of prophecy, death, or cosmic revelation
- **Inverted/distorted** when Vecna or V-Mardov forces are present
- In **Sprocket's theme specifically**, since he carries the Raven Queen's Cloak

The motif should be simple enough to hum: a descending minor phrase, five notes, with an unexpected resolution on the fourth. Think the Shire theme from Lord of the Rings in its versatility, but haunting rather than warm.

### Diegetic vs Non-Diegetic

| Type | Source | Style | Characters |
|------|--------|-------|------------|
| **Diegetic** | Bards performing in-world | Period-appropriate acoustic instruments | Randal (singer-songwriter guitar), Frozen Song (lute), tavern musicians |
| **Non-diegetic** | Score/soundtrack | Modern cinematic orchestral with genre infusions | All other character themes |

When a diegetic performer plays, the non-diegetic score should pull back -- letting the in-world music breathe. Transitions between diegetic and non-diegetic should be seamless: a tavern lute melody might gradually acquire orchestral strings beneath it as the scene shifts from casual to dramatic.

## Character Theme Specifications

### Daina Fireforge -- Heavy Metal

**Genre**: Heavy metal / doom metal
**Primary Instruments**: Distorted electric guitar (down-tuned), double bass drum, power chords, anvil strikes
**Tempo Range**: 100-140 BPM (slower doom riffs when brooding, faster thrash when raging)
**Key/Mode**: D minor (drop D tuning for guitar -- the metal standard)
**Mood**: Relentless, volcanic, grieving, unstoppable, forged-in-fire

**How It Integrates**: Daina's metal guitars enter beneath the orchestral strings during combat. When she rages, the orchestra drops out and raw distorted guitar takes over -- then the orchestra crashes back in on the downbeat of her strike. The Raven Queen flute appears as a clean melodic line above her distortion during emotional moments (her father's memory, the Fireforge revelation).

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. Daina does not make music. She IS the music -- the sound of a hammer on an anvil, the roar of a forge at full heat.

**Reference Style**: Think the heaviness of Black Sabbath's "Iron Man" crossed with the orchestral doom of Bear McCreary's God of War combat themes. The lava/forge aesthetic from her visual design should be audible -- grinding, molten, immense.

**Character-Specific Details**: The anvil strike is literal. Her theme should incorporate metalworking percussion (hammer on anvil, ringing steel) as rhythmic elements. Her Reborn transformation gets a key change -- same riff, shifted up a half step, with added harmonic overtones representing the glowing runes.

### Sprocket Overspark -- Electronic

**Genre**: Electronic / chiptune / glitch
**Primary Instruments**: Synthesizers, arpeggiated sequences, 8-bit textures, glitchy processing, theremin
**Tempo Range**: 130-160 BPM (frenetic, ticking, always accelerating)
**Key/Mode**: C major shifting to C minor (starts bright, darkens as fate closes in)
**Mood**: Manic, brilliant, ticking-clock, chaotic-good, cosmically burdened

**How It Integrates**: Sprocket's electronic textures layer beneath the orchestral score as a persistent rhythmic undercurrent -- like machinery humming under a cathedral. When he casts divination magic, the electronics surge forward. The Raven Queen flute motif is sampled, pitch-shifted, and looped inside his theme -- fate rendered as data.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. His theme is the sound of a mind that processes reality faster than reality moves.

**Reference Style**: Think Disasterpeace (Fez, It Follows) for the chiptune-meets-cosmic-dread quality. The ticking clock element evokes the "ultimate answer" -- his foreknowledge of his own death. Also draws from Undertale's Toby Fox for the way chiptune can carry devastating emotional weight.

**Character-Specific Details**: His Portent dice should have a sound -- a crystalline chime that precedes a key change, signaling that fate has been altered. His crystalline wings get ascending arpeggio flourishes. The maid-outfit-building montage gets a jaunty 8-bit interlude.

### Brigit Wildfire -- Celtic Music

**Genre**: Celtic / Irish folk
**Primary Instruments**: Uilleann pipes, bodhr&aacute;n, tin whistle, fiddle, Celtic harp
**Tempo Range**: 80-130 BPM (slow airs for emotional moments, reels for action)
**Key/Mode**: D Dorian (the quintessential Celtic mode -- minor feel with a raised 6th)
**Mood**: Rooted, fierce, nurturing, transformative, ancient

**How It Integrates**: Brigit's Celtic instruments are the most naturally compatible with the dark fantasy orchestral throughline. Her uilleann pipes can double the orchestral oboe lines. Her fiddle weaves into the string section. This makes her theme the emotional glue of the score -- always present, always grounding. When she transforms into her Progenitor form, the Celtic instruments distort: the fiddle becomes dissonant, the pipes become a wailing drone, the bodhr&aacute;n becomes thunderous.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. Though her fire-tending and campfire scenes create natural acoustic spaces where the Celtic instruments feel almost diegetic.

**Reference Style**: Think Bear McCreary's Outlander score for the Celtic orchestral fusion. Keyleth's motifs from Critical Role (Sam Riegel's compositions). The Progenitor transformation should evoke Wardruna -- ancient Nordic/Celtic ritual music that feels pre-human.

**Character-Specific Details**: As the player with 107+ session notes, Brigit is the narrator. Her theme should be the most developed, the most varied, the one that appears in the most contexts. The Zodiac Locket gets its own sub-motif within her theme -- a music-box-like Celtic harp figure that plays the Raven Queen motif in a new key. Her Stag form gets low uilleann pipe drones with deep bodhr&aacute;n pulse.

**Tempo Range**: 70-110 BPM (steady, patient, watchful)

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. His theme is the sound of watching a sunrise alone and knowing you might not see the next one.

**Reference Style**: Think early Fleetwood Mac (the Lindsey Buckingham fingerpicking era), blended with Nick Drake's melancholic acoustic work. The "Knight of the Dusken Wood" transformation echoes Hozier -- warm voice-like guitar lines that carry grief without breaking.

**Character-Specific Details**: His cyan-teal arrows should have a musical signature -- a harmonic overtone on the guitar that shimmers when an arrow is nocked. The Vodram flashback gets the same theme played in a minor key with reverb, like hearing it from the other side of a destroyed world.

### Nocturnus Wayne -- Emo

**Genre**: Emo / post-hardcore / gothic rock
**Primary Instruments**: Distorted guitar (gain cranked), bass (driving eighth notes), crash cymbals, piano (sparse, in breakdowns)
**Tempo Range**: 90-140 BPM (brooding verses, explosive choruses)
**Key/Mode**: B-flat minor (dark, dramatic, the key of gothic romance)
**Mood**: Brooding, explosive, self-destructive, reluctantly noble, razor-edged

**How It Integrates**: Nocturnus's emo guitars clash against the orchestral throughline -- deliberately. His theme is the one that disrupts the score's cohesion, because he disrupts the party's cohesion. When he cooperates (Slow Fall to save Daina, diamond to resurrect Varis), his theme briefly harmonizes with the orchestra before pulling away. The Raven Queen flute plays in counterpoint to his distorted guitar -- fate and rebellion in dialogue.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. His theme is the sound of what Kingdom Hearts would be if Sora lost.

**Reference Style**: My Chemical Romance's "The Black Parade" for the gothic theatrical quality. The Used for the raw, ragged vocal-like guitar tone. For the Empty Body moments (when he becomes translucent and young), switch to Radiohead's "Exit Music" -- ethereal, fragile, heartbreaking.

**Character-Specific Details**: Bane (his sentient sword) should have its own sub-motif: a low, resonant hum like a cello played with too much pressure, always slightly out of tune. The Nightmare Sword corruption gets atonal, feedback-laden distortion. The pacifying spore covering half his face gets a split-channel effect: emo guitar in one ear, organic ambient drone in the other. "I FEEL LIKE A GOD" (absorbing Asmodeus's essence) gets the full My Chemical Romance treatment -- blast beats, screaming guitar, and then sudden silence.

### Nyx Khamestria -- Classical Ballet

**Genre**: Classical ballet / romantic-era orchestral
**Primary Instruments**: Solo violin, celesta, harp, strings (pizzicato for playful moments, legato for emotional)
**Tempo Range**: 60-120 BPM (waltz time signatures, 3/4 and 6/8)
**Key/Mode**: F-sharp minor (delicate, melancholic, faintly otherworldly)
**Mood**: Graceful, melancholic, defiant, fey-touched, heartbreaking

**How It Integrates**: Nyx's ballet theme is the most "classical" of the character themes, which means it blends invisibly into the orchestral throughline -- and that invisibility IS her character. She hides in plain sight. Her solo violin surfaces when she is alone or when her true feelings emerge. The celesta represents her fey magic (involuntary, leaking, unsettling). The Raven Queen flute harmonizes naturally with her violin -- two solo instruments trading phrases like sisters who were separated.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. The Knight of the Flower Field persona gets a brighter, major-key variant that sounds almost diegetic -- like music from a children's storybook -- but it is the lie she tells the world.

**Reference Style**: Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake for the duality (white swan/black swan = Nyx/Xyn). Saint-Saens' "The Swan" for the solo cello/violin melancholy. Studio Ghibli's Joe Hisaishi for the celesta work (Spirited Away's "One Summer's Day"). The fey magic manifesting gets Debussy-like whole-tone scales -- nothing resolves, everything shimmers.

**Character-Specific Details**: Her owl Hedwig's death (Ch. 8) should establish a two-note motif (descending minor second) that recurs whenever loss is present. The Xyn connection gets a mirror of Nyx's theme played in inversion -- the same notes, upside down, heard through a portal. Reynardine's presence warps Nyx's theme into a music-box version that slows and distorts.

### Bergs Fireforge -- Psychedelic Rock

**Genre**: Psychedelic rock / space rock / prog
**Primary Instruments**: Fuzz guitar, organ (Hammond B3), wah pedal, bongo drums, sitar (for the myconid/fungal consciousness)
**Tempo Range**: 70-120 BPM (slow, expansive, hypnotic)
**Key/Mode**: E Mixolydian (major-ish but with a flat 7th -- earthy, unresolved, cosmic)
**Mood**: Earthy, expansive, connected, transcendent, dwarf-who-became-a-god

**How It Integrates**: Bergs' psychedelic guitars create a warm, fuzzy haze beneath the orchestral strings -- like looking at a cathedral through heat shimmer. His theme starts grounded and dwarven (heavy, rhythmic, forge-adjacent) but acquires increasingly cosmic textures as his arc progresses. By the Stone Table sacrifice, the psychedelic rock has fully merged with the orchestral throughline -- he IS the score. The Raven Queen flute plays through a phaser effect in his sections.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. His theme is the sound of mushroom spores carrying consciousness across a continent.

**Reference Style**: Pink Floyd's "Echoes" for the cosmic-consciousness quality. Black Sabbath's "Planet Caravan" for the gentle, spacey side of heavy music. Beatles' "Tomorrow Never Knows" for the sitar/drone elements. Post-ascension, add Tame Impala's modern psychedelic production -- lush, swirling, enveloping.

**Character-Specific Details**: The myconid network connection gets a bioluminescent audio texture -- a sustained drone with pulsing, breathing rhythm (like the spores that drift in his wake). Kelemtor (the talking skull) gets a comedic surf-rock riff. The buddy cop duo with Sprocket gets their themes mashed up -- fuzz guitar meets chiptune. The Stone Table ascension should be the score's most psychedelic moment: full Pink Floyd space-rock breakdown dissolving into pure orchestral light.

### Randal -- Singer-Songwriter Guitar (DIEGETIC)

**Genre**: Singer-songwriter / acoustic folk
**Primary Instruments**: Acoustic guitar (fingerpicked and strummed), panflute, voice (implied/hummed), hand drums
**Tempo Range**: 60-100 BPM (unhurried, contemplative, dying-man's-pace)
**Key/Mode**: C major / A minor (the most accessible keys -- music for everyone)
**Mood**: Gentle, wise, urgent-beneath-calm, mortal, watching-the-clock

**How It Integrates**: Randal's theme is DIEGETIC -- he is a bard, and his music exists in the world. When Randal plays, the non-diegetic orchestral score fades to silence, letting his acoustic guitar fill the space. This makes his performances feel sacred -- the world pauses to listen. When his counter-charm wakes Brigit from a mind-flayer stun (Ch. 30), his diegetic guitar seamlessly becomes the non-diegetic score as the scene shifts from intimate to epic.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: **Diegetic.** Randal performs in-world. His lantern staff and traveling minstrel aesthetic are acoustic. Other characters can hear him play.

**Reference Style**: Nick Drake for the fingerpicked melancholy. Iron and Wine for the gentle warmth. The panflute evokes Andean folk music -- high altitude, thin air, spiritual weight. For his "God of Choice" divine nature, add a subtle overtone singing quality (Tuvan throat singing) -- one voice containing multitudes.

**Character-Specific Details**: His dimensional shackles should produce a faint rhythmic clink that becomes part of his music's percussion. His dying status means his theme should imperceptibly slow across the campaign -- same melody, incrementally fewer BPM. The "God of Choice" reveal gets a moment where his simple acoustic guitar suddenly resonates with impossible harmonics, as though a single instrument is producing the sound of many.

### Josh Coolbreeze -- Heavy Woodwind Orchestral

**Genre**: Heavy woodwind orchestral / wind ensemble
**Primary Instruments**: Bassoon, contrabassoon, bass clarinet, French horn, tuba, timpani
**Tempo Range**: 60-100 BPM (heavy, deliberate, weighty)
**Key/Mode**: E-flat minor (deep, sonorous, the key of brass gravitas)
**Mood**: Titanic, ominous, godslayer, world-ending-consequence, cool-name-terrifying-power

**How It Integrates**: Josh Coolbreeze's heavy woodwind theme is the most purely orchestral of all character themes -- it does not clash with the throughline, it OVERWHELMS it. When Josh acts (killing Asmodeus in Ch. 136), the standard orchestral forces are not enough. The entire woodwind section doubles down: bassoons in their lowest register, contrabassoons vibrating the subwoofer, French horns in unison. The Raven Queen flute motif is consumed by the larger woodwind mass -- fate swallowed by consequence.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. His theme is the sound of a god dying -- the air leaving the room, the pressure dropping.

**Reference Style**: Holst's "Mars" from The Planets for the militant heavy brass/woodwind quality. Hans Zimmer's Inception (BRAAAM) for the sub-bass impact. John Williams' Imperial March for the sense that something enormous is approaching. The name "Coolbreeze" is ironic -- his theme should feel like a hurricane.

**Character-Specific Details**: The killing of Asmodeus gets the score's single loudest moment -- every woodwind and brass instrument in unison on one chord, held for four beats, then absolute silence. The three specters escaping should get three descending woodwind runs (high to low) -- the sound of divine power scattering.

### Reynaldine -- Piano Live Ballad

**Genre**: Piano ballad / romantic classical / art song
**Primary Instruments**: Grand piano (solo), strings (entering gradually), voice (implied -- the melody is singable)
**Tempo Range**: 50-80 BPM (rubato, emotionally responsive, never metronomic)
**Key/Mode**: D-flat major (the key of Chopin nocturnes -- intimate, reflective, rich)
**Mood**: Manipulative, beautiful, tragic, four-beings-in-one, the-most-dangerous-music-in-the-score

**How It Integrates**: Reynaldine's piano is seductive. It enters the orchestral throughline as a solo voice -- delicate, beautiful, trustworthy. Then strings join. Then you realize the piano has been subtly altering the key of every other theme playing beneath it. Reynaldine's theme is the one that changes OTHER themes. The Raven Queen flute avoids his piano -- when Reynaldine plays, the flute goes silent. Fate does not enter this room.

**Diegetic/Non-diegetic**: Non-diegetic. The piano is Reynaldine's true voice -- the four beings (Juliannia, Graz'zt, Gabriel, Reynardine) each contribute a voice to the texture. The left hand is Graz'zt (low, rumbling). The right hand melody alternates between Gabriel (ascending, angelic) and Juliannia (descending, fey). Reynardine is the sustain pedal -- connecting everything, blurring the boundaries.

**Reference Style**: Chopin's Ballade No. 1 in G minor for the narrative arc within a single piece. Ryuichi Sakamoto (Merry Christmas Mr. Lawrence) for the devastating simplicity. For the four-beings-revealed moment, the piano splits into four independent voices like a Bach fugue -- beautiful and terrifying.

**Character-Specific Details**: Nyx's theme distorts into a music-box version when Reynaldine is present. The "Some things are worth fighting for" line should be a piano phrase that directly quotes and inverts Randal's pacifist melody. The plane-collapse attempt gets the piano playing impossibly fast (like Liszt's transcendental etudes) -- technical virtuosity as cosmic ambition.

## Location Themes

### Tavern (Argos, WindHaven, general)

**Palette**: Acoustic guitar, fiddle, bodhr&aacute;n, tin whistle, lute
**Tempo**: 100-130 BPM (lively)
**Mode**: G Mixolydian (bright, festive, warm)
**Feel**: Warm amber light rendered as sound. Think Witcher 3 tavern music -- the campfire glow of civilization. Diegetic musicians are present. Randal and Frozen Song may be performing. The Raven Queen flute appears only as a distant echo from outside the tavern walls.

### Dungeon / Underdark

**Palette**: Low strings (col legno, sul ponticello), prepared piano, waterphone, subharmonic bass
**Tempo**: 40-70 BPM (crawling, oppressive)
**Mode**: Locrian (the most dissonant mode -- nothing feels resolved)
**Feel**: Silence is the primary instrument. Sound design dominates: dripping water, distant echoes, the groan of stone. When music enters, it is sparse and wrong -- intervals too wide, rhythm too irregular. Think Akira Yamaoka (Silent Hill) for the industrial-horror texture. The bioluminescent blue-green of the Deep should be audible as high-frequency tonal shimmers.

### City (Argos, Cloud City, Magister's Circle)

**Palette**: Full orchestra (lighter), percussion (market bustle), bells, street musician elements
**Tempo**: 90-120 BPM (bustling)
**Mode**: D major (bright, civic, purposeful)
**Feel**: Layered -- multiple diegetic sources (market vendors, street performers, church bells) competing beneath a non-diegetic orchestral line. Each city has a sub-variant: Argos is saltier (accordion, concertina), Cloud City is airier (glass harmonica, high strings, wind sounds), the Magister's Circle is more academic (harpsichord, chamber music).

### Feywild

**Palette**: Celesta, harp glissandi, reversed/time-stretched instruments, glass harmonica, detuned music box
**Tempo**: Variable (time moves differently -- the tempo should breathe, accelerate, and decelerate unpredictably)
**Mode**: Whole-tone scale (nothing resolves, everything floats)
**Feel**: Beautiful and wrong. Every instrument should sound slightly off -- a piano with one note out of tune, a harp with one string too tight. Colors are oversaturated, and the music should feel the same. Think Joe Hisaishi (Spirited Away) meets Debussy's "Prelude to the Afternoon of a Faun." Bergs' psychedelic rock elements bleed through here post-ascension.

### Forest (Evallan, general wilderness)

**Palette**: Uilleann pipes, nature sounds (integrated rhythmically), woodwinds, solo cello, gentle percussion
**Tempo**: 70-100 BPM (breathing, organic)
**Mode**: D Dorian (Brigit's key -- the forest is her domain)
**Feel**: Brigit's Celtic theme is the foundation. Layers of nature sound design (birdsong, wind, creek water) become rhythmic elements. Think the quieter moments of the Witcher 3 Skellige music. The Zodiac Locket motif appears when the forest is ancient or magical.

### Ocean / Ship (Thornberry Shade)

**Palette**: Accordion/concertina, boomy low drums, shanty-style vocals (implied), fiddle, crashing wave percussion
**Tempo**: 80-110 BPM (rolling, wave-paced -- 6/8 time signature)
**Mode**: A Dorian (sea shanty key, minor but moving forward)
**Feel**: The Thornberry Shade is home to both Group A and Group B. The sea theme should carry Nyx's fey shimmer (she glamours the ship), Varis's watchful guitar (he keeps watch), and the general party warmth. Think Hans Zimmer's Pirates of the Caribbean stripped of bombast -- the human moments between storms.

### Vag'Mahar / Dwarven Stronghold

**Palette**: Anvil percussion, low brass, war drums, chanting (implied), hammering rhythms
**Tempo**: 80-100 BPM (marching, relentless)
**Mode**: D minor (Daina's key -- the forge's domain)
**Feel**: Daina and Bergs' themes converge here. The forge is audible -- rhythmic hammering as percussion, the roar of bellows as sustained bass. Think the Mines of Moria theme from Lord of the Rings but with more heat and industry. The Fireforge legacy made audible.

## Combat Music

### Standard Encounter

**Palette**: Full orchestra + character themes of combatants woven in
**Tempo**: 120-140 BPM
**Mode**: Varies by enemy type (undead = Phrygian, beasts = Aeolian, humanoid = minor keys)
**Structure**: 8-bar intro (tension build) -> main theme (combat proper) -> dynamic sections that shift based on who is fighting. Daina's guitar enters when she rages. Sprocket's electronics surge when he casts. The combat theme should be modular -- pieces that can be mixed based on which PCs are engaged.

### Boss Fight

**Palette**: Full orchestra at maximum force + choir (implied, wordless) + the antagonist's specific instrumentation
**Tempo**: 100-130 BPM (heavier, more deliberate than standard combat)
**Mode**: Tritone-heavy (the "devil's interval" -- dissonance as threat)
**Structure**: Three phases. Phase 1: the boss theme dominates, party themes suppressed. Phase 2: party themes begin to push through. Phase 3: party themes overtake the boss theme in a musical victory.

**Boss-specific instrumentation**:
- **Mordred/Ramses**: Pipe organ + distorted choir -- lich magic as corrupted religion
- **Oblivion Wayne**: Nocturnus's emo theme inverted and slowed -- his family turned against him
- **Peraxis Phyrexia**: Illithid psychic static -- granular synthesis, brain-scrambling frequencies
- **Asmodeus**: Josh Coolbreeze's woodwind theme, but Josh's version is louder -- the god dies to the bigger sound
- **Reynaldine (four beings)**: Four piano voices playing different pieces simultaneously

### Chase Scene

**Palette**: Driving percussion, ostinato strings, character themes at double tempo
**Tempo**: 140-170 BPM (breathless)
**Mode**: Mixolydian (forward momentum without full resolution)
**Feel**: Think the Lupin III chase themes or Mission Impossible -- genre-agnostic excitement. Sprocket's chiptune and Tesca's tabla provide the rhythmic backbone. Comedy chase scenes get faster tempos and major keys. Life-or-death chases get minor keys and dissonance.

## Emotional Cues

### Victory

**Palette**: Full orchestra, brass fanfare, Raven Queen flute (triumphant variant), all active character themes in harmony
**Tempo**: 100-120 BPM (measured, earned)
**Key**: D major (the "victory key" -- bright, warm, satisfying)
**Note**: Victory in Mardov always costs something. The fanfare should contain a minor-key shadow -- a single oboe line that mourns beneath the triumph.

### Loss / Death

**Palette**: Solo instrument (whichever character is affected), Raven Queen flute (dirge variant), sustained strings
**Tempo**: 50-60 BPM (funereal)
**Key**: F minor (grief)
**Note**: The Raven Queen motif is most prominent during death scenes. It should feel like a hand reaching down -- not threatening, but inevitable. Sabine Ra chose this. She chose them.

### Revelation / Prophecy

**Palette**: Raven Queen flute (foreground, unaccompanied), then gradual orchestral swell, celesta, glass harmonica
**Tempo**: Free time / rubato (time stops during revelation)
**Key**: Whole-tone transition -> resolving to the relevant character's key
**Note**: The three prophecies (Ruth, the Hags, Mia) each get their own variant. Ruth's is warm (gold arcane geometry). The Hags' is corrupt (organic purple wisps). Mia's is cosmic (indigo star-light).

### Romance / Intimacy

**Palette**: Solo acoustic instruments from the relevant characters' themes, minimal orchestration
**Tempo**: 60-80 BPM (heartbeat)
**Key**: Whatever key brings the two characters' themes into closest harmony
**Note**: Romance in Mardov is rare and earned. Sprocket and Trixie get a chiptune waltz. Nyx and her lost sister get the inverted mirror theme. Brigit and the forest get a Celtic lullaby.

### Betrayal

**Palette**: The betrayer's theme, distorted and played against the victim's theme in dissonance
**Tempo**: Decelerating (time slowing as trust breaks)
**Key**: The victim's key, with the betrayer's key modulating against it
**Note**: Dallor's betrayals get Stalwart's country fiddle played out of tune. Reynaldine's manipulations get his piano altering other themes' keys without permission. The party split (Ch. 41) gets every character theme playing simultaneously and then separating into silence.

## Production Notes

### Instrument Priority for MusicGen/AI Generation

When generating tracks, prioritize recognizable genre markers:
1. The character's primary instrument (it must be identifiable within 5 seconds)
2. The Raven Queen flute motif (woven in where specified)
3. The dark fantasy orchestral throughline (strings + brass foundation)
4. Genre-specific percussion and rhythm

### Mixing Philosophy

- Character themes should be mixed to emerge from and recede into the orchestral throughline
- Never more than 2-3 character themes active simultaneously
- The Raven Queen flute sits in the mid-high frequency range and should always cut through
- Diegetic music (Randal, tavern) gets a different reverb space (room) vs non-diegetic (hall/plate)

### Campaign Arc Across Score

| Act | Musical Character | Orchestral Weight |
|-----|-------------------|-------------------|
| Act 1 | Simple, folk-leaning, themes introduced one at a time | Light chamber orchestra |
| Act 2 | Themes begin interacting, sea/voyage textures | Medium orchestra, nautical elements |
| Act 3 | Split: two parallel scores diverge | Full orchestra split into two palettes (blue-green vs amber) |
| Act 4 | Themes converge, harmonic tension builds | Full orchestra unified, increasing dissonance |
| Act 5 | All themes active, cosmic scale | Maximum orchestral force + all genre elements simultaneously |
