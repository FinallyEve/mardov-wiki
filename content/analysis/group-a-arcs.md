# Group A — The Dwarves / Craftkeepers

> Group A is the underground track: the characters who descended into the Deep after the party split in Chapter 41, spending months in bioluminescent tunnels, Vag'Mahar politics, and Underdark warfare before emerging at the Reunion. In production docs, they are defined by their cool blue-green palette -- armor and skin tinted by months of underground bioluminescence, faces sharper, colors cooled.

**Other names**: The Dwarves (Act 3), The Craftkeepers (Act 4), The Good Guys Guild (Act 4, Ch 101+)

**Core members**: Daina Fireforge, Bergs Fireforge, Nocturnus Wayne
**Frequent allies**: Birt (solo mission Ch 75), Kelemtor (carried by Bergs), Bartrim Hadrin's Brezerkers

## Members

### Daina (Diesa) Fireforge

- **Player**: Jasmine
- **Class/Race**: Barbarian, Hill Dwarf (reincarnated as Duergar in Ch 87)
- **First Appearance**: Chapter 0 (original Scarlet Tunic Brigade intake)
- **Aliases**: Daina Reborn, The Forge, The Force, Paragon of the Warforged

#### Arc Summary

Daina enters the campaign as a vengeful loner -- a red-haired dwarf raised in exile, hunting the black-eyed red-bearded stranger who poisoned her father Thorkon Fireforge. She knows nothing of her Fireforge heritage, nothing of the Bronzebeard dynasty she was exiled from, nothing of the royal weaponsmithing legacy she carries. Her arc is the slow discovery of all of it -- and the transformation from avenger to leader, from the woman who hits everything to the woman whose heartbeat commands an army.

The Dwarves track forces her into the politics of Vag'Mahar, the very city her family was exiled from. She discovers the Forgesworn legacy, learns her father was a royal weaponsmith, and uncovers the conspiracy (Ghost-Eye Thorrin's betrayal) that destroyed her family. Her reincarnation as a Duergar during the Ritual of Reincarnation (Ch 87) transforms her physically -- platinum hair, glowing amber eyes, dark purple-grey skin, armor covered in orange runic inscriptions that pulse like a heartbeat.

The Anchor of War ascension (Ch 104-105) is her defining moment: she becomes the living embodiment of war itself, commanding warforged through her heartbeat, wielding the Anchor as both weapon and authority. Her declaration of war on the gods (Ch 106) stakes the party's position against divine authority. She literally moves a mountain (Ch 107).

## Group Dynamics and Themes

### The Underground Transformation

Group A's defining characteristic is that they go underground and come back changed. The production notes capture this visually: when they emerge at the Reunion, their armor and skin are tinted by months of bioluminescent blue-green, their colors cooled, their faces sharper. They have been shaped by darkness, pressure, and proximity to the earth itself.

This is not just visual. Daina enters the underground as an avenger and emerges as a Paragon. Bergs enters as a covert agent and emerges as a craftmaster connected to fungal consciousness. Nocturnus enters as a vampire and emerges as... still a vampire, but one the Dwarves track has channeled into something productive.

### The Fireforge Legacy

Group A is bound by dwarven heritage more than the other tracks. Both Daina and Bergs are Fireforges, connected to the Magna Har, the First Forge, and the Bronzebeard dynasty. Their parallel discovery of this heritage -- Daina through vengeance for her father, Bergs through the Artificer's Guild mission -- is the campaign's most sustained mystery arc.

The Fireforge connection drives the key Group A moments: Dvinter Castle (Ch 37-38), the Vag'Mahar political arc (Ch 42-66), the Anchor of War ascension (Ch 104-105), and the Stone Table sacrifice (Ch 132).

### Light vs. Dark

Group A is the campaign's exploration of darkness as a tool rather than a curse. Nocturnus is literally a creature of darkness. The Underdark is literal darkness. Vag'Mahar's political corruption is metaphorical darkness. But Group A produces the campaign's brightest outcomes: Daina's Reborn transformation (forge-light glowing from within), Bergs' godhood (warmth entering rooms with him), and Nocturnus' fulfillment of the Darkest Night prophecy (darkness completing its purpose).

### The Lone Wolf Paradox

Bergs' mantra -- "there is no such thing as a lone wolf" -- directly contradicts Nocturnus' entire identity. And yet both are proven right. Bergs ascends through connection (myconid network, Stone Table sacrifice, community). Nocturnus ascends through isolation perfected (solo Tiamat mission, absorbing Asmodeus alone). The Group A thesis is that both paths lead to the same destination -- but Bergs' path is less painful.
